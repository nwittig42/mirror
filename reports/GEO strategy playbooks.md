# Turn every audit gap into a sourced fix

GEO agencies build strategies by tracing what AI engines cite rather than by improving content in general. For each buyer question and each engine they record who got named, which URLs the answer cited, and which competitors appeared, and each to-do then comes from one specific gap, such as "Perplexity cited this G2 grid and this Reddit thread for 'best X for Y', the competitor is on both and you are on neither." The leading platforms (Peec, Profound, LLM Pulse, AthenaHQ) all use this prompt × engine → citation → gap → action model, and split the work into **on-site** (owned pages) and **off-site** (editorial, review and user-generated sources). Three findings from the evidence should shape a product that writes these lists automatically. First, **the strongest controlled evidence favors substance over format**: an explicit price, specs, comparisons and a recent date drive citation, while headings, schema and llms.txt barely move it. Second, **off-site brand mentions correlate with AI visibility about three times more strongly than backlinks**, so the cited third-party URLs in an audit are the outreach list. Third, **no engine has a working correction form for business facts**, so every wrong fact has to be fixed at its source and then re-tested on a schedule. Almost all of the numbers below come from vendors that sell GEO, SEO or listings tools, and no tactic has a controlled, live-engine causal study. A credible product therefore grades every task by its evidence and promises re-measurement, not rankings.

**Evidence grades used in this report:** **A** = official documentation or a controlled experiment; **B** = a large correlational or vendor study with a stated method; **C** = practitioner or agency claim, self-reported case, or secondhand figure; **Myth** = tested and not supported.

## Agencies all run one loop, and credibility comes from linking each task to its evidence

The platforms' "recommendations" features are close to interchangeable. Peec's Actions split gaps into On-Page and Off-Page, cluster cited sources by type ("editorial listicles", "Reddit discussions", "product pages"), and rank each with a **Relative Opportunity Score of 1-3** that weighs how often engines cite a source type against how much competitors dominate it ([Peec](https://peec.ai/product-actions)). Peec also added a "Source Gap Analysis" view and a robots.txt "Site Crawlability" action in 2026 ([Peec changelog](https://peec.ai/changelog)). Profound's Actions produce **content briefs, not finished work**: headings, secondary keywords, and "specific stats and facts that AI is already citing" ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/introducing-actions)). LLM Pulse publishes the most complete to-do design found. **Every recommendation links to the queries, citations or threads behind it.** Items carry high/medium/low priority and an estimated impact, users can mark them completed or archived, thin data produces a short list rather than filler, and an AI step classifies the business's size so "a global enterprise gets PR and original-data plays rather than small-business outreach" ([LLM Pulse](https://llmpulse.ai/help-center/recommendations)). Users can also set limits such as "no competitor comparisons" or "no paid placements" (same source).

Agency engagements follow the same arc. Superlines' published 90-day plan spends days 1-30 on a buyer-stage prompt set tracked per platform, a "source ecosystem" map, and a technical check. Days 31-60 fix existing high-value pages first (product, pricing, comparison, docs) and correct outdated third-party profiles. Days 61-90 re-measure against the baseline after **3-4 weeks of trend data**. Its prioritization factors are business value, buyer stage, current visibility, competitor advantage, size of the information gap, number of platforms affected, and difficulty of the fix ([Superlines](https://superlines.io/articles/90-day-geo-plan-ai-search-visibility)). Discovered Labs and others publish near-identical 30/60/90 plans, and one adds an escalation checkpoint if metrics haven't moved by day 45 ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/blog/geo-timeline-when-to-expect-results-30-60-90-day-benchmarks/); [upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/geo-first-90-days-what-to-expect)). Perrill sells this roadmap on its own for **$8,500** ([Perrill](https://www.perrill.com/?p=170229)). Agencies promise first citations in roughly 30-60 days and treat anyone promising results within two weeks as a red flag (grade C).

The criticisms of the category show what "specific" has to mean. Reviewers say most tools "help teams understand the problem, while few help them execute" ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/why-most-geo-tools-fail-at-ai-search-visibility-and-what-actually-works)). HubSpot's free grader says its own advice is AI-generated and not reviewed by a human, and third parties call it broad ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/aeo-grader?faq=5)). One buyer's guide names the red flags: guaranteed ChatGPT ranks, single-screenshot "proof", unexplained prompt selection, and treating a mention as a citation. It also warns that **"being mentioned wrongly is not a win"** ([SBO.sg](https://sbo.sg/business/marketing/geo-monitoring-tools-agencies-ai-visibility/)). A task is credibly specific when it names the lost prompt, the engine, the competitor that won, the cited URL, and the exact asset to change. It should also show the reasoning behind its priority and carry a re-check date.

## Each engine has a different gate, so one fix rarely covers all seven

The engines draw on different indexes, so a task that helps one may do nothing for another. **ChatGPT** live search requires OAI-SearchBot access ("sites that are opted out... will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers"), and robots.txt changes take effect in about 24 hours. Blocking GPTBot only opts the site out of training (grade A, [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** require nothing beyond being indexed and eligible for a snippet: Google says no special schema, AI text files or markup are needed (grade A, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). **Claude** appears to search through Brave (grade B, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/21/anthropic-appears-to-be-using-brave-to-power-web-searches-for-its-claude-chatbot/)), and one vendor reports that 79% of Claude-cited URLs sit in Brave's top 10 (grade C, [oltre.ai](https://www.oltre.ai/en/blog/how-claude-picks-sources-technical-breakdown-claude-citations/)). **Copilot** runs on Bing, and Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report (preview since February 2026) shows grounding queries and citation share. That makes it the only engine with first-party citation data ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039)). **Perplexity** licenses Yelp and Tripadvisor data for local answers ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/perplexity-enhances-ai-search-engine-with-direct-yelp-data-integration/); [Skift](https://skift.com/2025/01/14/tripadvisor-exec-explains-partnership-with-chatgpt-competitor-perplexity/)). **Gemini** uses Google Business Profile data without citing it ([Yext](https://www.yext.com/research/articles/ai-citations-user-locations-query-context/)). **Grok** has separate web and X search tools, but no citation-mix data exists for it.

Two engine behaviors change how to prioritize work. First, **most ChatGPT answers never search**: Ahrefs found only **14.4%** of 66.6M logged-out responses triggered a web search, concluding that "visibility depends on what ChatGPT already knows about a brand" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/)). Semrush puts the figure at 34.5% ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-search-insights/)). Graphite found that forcing search in prompt tracking shifts measured visibility by about 20 points ([Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/do-not-force-search-prompt-tracking)). Strategy therefore runs on two tracks. The **retrieval track** (AI Overviews, AI Mode, Perplexity, Copilot, ChatGPT with search) responds in days to weeks. The **memory track** (ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini answering without search) moves only when a new model ships. Local and commercial prompts search more often, so local businesses live mostly on the retrieval track, while a SaaS brand asked about generically depends more on memory.

Second, **engines rewrite each prompt into sub-searches, and ranking for those predicts citation better than ranking for the original prompt**. ChatGPT's fan-outs added "2026" 22.9% of the time, "official" 13.1% and "price" 8.9%, and 6.8% were `site:` searches of brand domains ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/)). Only 38% of AI Overview-cited pages rank in the top 10. Surfer reports that pages ranking for fan-out queries were **161% more likely** to be cited (grade C, [summary](https://www.ekamoira.com/blog/query-fan-out-original-research-on-how-ai-search-multiplies-every-query-and-why-most-brands-are-invisible)), and only 19% of AI Mode citations come from the top-20 results ([seoClarity](https://www.seoclarity.net/ai-mode-rankings-overlap)). The "AI search queries" field in an audit is therefore the most direct map of which pages need to exist. Sources also turn over quickly. ChatGPT's Reddit citations fell 86% in a few days in August 2026 ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/reddits-citations-in-chatgpt-fall/)), and Reddit, Wikipedia and YouTube citations dropped 88%, 80% and 27% between March and September 2026 as official sources gained. For factual questions, **84.4%** of answers cited at least one official source ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt/)). Older "ChatGPT loves Reddit and Wikipedia" rankings are out of date, and a gap list has to be rebuilt per engine and per scan. ChatGPT and Perplexity share only about 11% of their cited sources (grade C, [The Rank Masters](https://www.therankmasters.com/insights/ai-visibility/ai-citation-gap-analysis-tools)).

## On-site work that matters: prices, specs, comparisons and dates, not formatting

The agency claim that adding statistics, quotes and citations raises visibility "up to 40%" comes from the 2024 Princeton GEO paper, and it is a **lab result that did not replicate**. In that paper the gains went mostly to low-ranked sources, and the top-ranked source *lost* 30% under one tactic. C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025) found only 3 of 54 method-domain tests significant, with gains shrinking as competitors adopted the same tricks. SAGEO Arena found that rewriting page bodies "for the LLM" *cut* retrieval presence by about 9% and final citation by about 6% ([critical survey, arXiv 2607.14035](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1); [arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)). The strongest controlled study ran about 252,000 trials across six LLMs (SIGIR 2026). It found four "gatekeeper" factors with odds ratios above 100 in every model: **topic mismatch, price not mentioned, an old rather than recent timestamp, and lower list position**. Missing specs, no comparisons, hedged language and internal contradictions also hurt. **Formatting changes had no effect** ([arXiv 2605.25517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25517)). The study tested pages already in the model's context, so it shows what wins once a page is retrieved, not how to get retrieved.

Placement within a page matters as well. ChatGPT takes **44.2% of its citations from the first 30% of a page**, and cited passages are about twice as likely to use plain definitional language (grade B, [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-citations-content-study-469483)). AI-cited URLs are about 25.7% fresher than organic results, ChatGPT most of all (grade B, [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/?pp=env)). The technical gate is the first HTML response: GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot download JavaScript but do not run it, so prices or services rendered client-side are invisible to them (grade B, server logs, [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)). That risk is highest for single-page SaaS marketing sites. Several popular tactics do not hold up. In a difference-in-differences test, adding JSON-LD schema produced **−4.6%** on AI Overviews and non-significant changes elsewhere, although that test covered only pages that were already cited ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)). **97% of llms.txt files got zero requests** ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/97-of-llms-txt-files-got-no-requests-ahrefs-data-shows/579478/)). The claim that a Wikidata item makes a brand "2.7x more likely" to be cited names no study. Self-published "best X" and comparison pages are still cited, but their ChatGPT share roughly halved after a 2026 model change (grade B, [Lily Ray](https://x.com/lilyraynyc/article/2090256147441746150)).

| On-site tactic | Grade | Local version | SaaS version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allow OAI-SearchBot, Bingbot, Googlebot, Perplexity/Claude search bots; check the CDN/WAF isn't blocking them | A | Same | Same |
| Key facts in server-rendered HTML (test with view-source/curl) | B | Services, prices, hours, NAP | Prices, plan limits, integrations |
| State an explicit price (no "contact us") | A (controlled) | "Starting at $X" on service pages | Public /pricing with numbers |
| Specs/service details and a comparison section | A (controlled) | Services offered and not offered | "X vs [competitor]" and "alternatives" pages with honest tables |
| Answer plus key facts in the first third, definitional sentence | B | Per service × city page | Per use-case page ("for [audience]") |
| Visible "last updated" date that matches real edits; old dates hurt | A/B | Quarterly refresh | Quarterly refresh of pricing and comparison pages |
| Remove contradictory facts across pages | A (controlled) | Old addresses and hours | Old pricing and feature pages (301 or noindex) |
| Schema (Organization/LocalBusiness, `sameAs`) | Hygiene only; no causal lift | Yes | Yes |
| FAQ blocks, question headings, tables | C | Covers more questions | Covers more questions |
| llms.txt, statistics-stuffing, "LLM-style" rewrites | Myth / can hurt | Skip | Skip |

Local sites differ in one important way. BrightLocal's study of about 200,000 non-branded local prompts found business websites made up **42% of citations and 93% of unique cited domains**, with Google Business Profile at 28.6% (mostly in Google's AI surfaces) and Yelp at about 9.5%. Only 20-33% of results overlapped between repeat runs ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)). That variability is why single-run audit results need repeated sampling before they become tasks.

## Off-site work: the URLs an engine already cites are the outreach list

Off-site signals carry the most measured weight. Across 75,000 brands, branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at **0.664 versus 0.218 for backlinks** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). A later cross-engine run found 0.66-0.71 for mentions on ChatGPT, AI Mode and AIO, about 0.74 for YouTube mentions, and backlinks "very weak" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)). When ChatGPT recommends a SaaS tool it cites the tool's own site only **11.6%** of the time ([DerivateX](https://derivatex.agency/report/b2b-saas-ai-citation-study/)). These are correlations, and no study shows that getting onto a source causes an engine to add a brand. The practical method follows from them anyway. Take every URL cited in answers where the business was missing, remove duplicates across prompts and engines, weight each by how many prompts and engines cite it, classify it, and assign the task template for its class.

Listicles are the largest class for discovery prompts. "Best X" lists were **43.8%** of the pages ChatGPT cited across 750 top-of-funnel prompts, brands ranked higher on a list were recommended more often, 79.1% of cited lists had been updated in 2025, and **35% sat on low-authority domains**, so small blogs count ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/)). For local businesses, Whitespark's 47-expert survey ranks **presence on curated best-of lists as the #1 AI-visibility factor**, followed by unstructured mentions on industry and news sites. It ranks NAP consistency (#31) and the number of directory citations (#40) much lower ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). Listings still make up a large share of local citations, 42% in Yext's 6.8M-citation study, so they are a baseline requirement rather than a growth lever ([Yext](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-citations-86-percent-of-sources-are-brand-managed)). Review volume alone is a weak lever: G2's own analysis found an **R² of about 0.009** between review count and LLM citations ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/do-more-g2-reviews-mean-more-ai-visibility)). Being present on the review sites that matter in the vertical counts for more. For B2B SaaS, a G2, Capterra or GetApp profile is the minimum to be considered, and GetApp was cited more than G2 in one 456,000-citation study ([Hall](https://usehall.com/guides/review-platform-ai-citation-analysis)). BrightLocal reports that 45% of US consumers used AI for a local recommendation in the past year ([BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/research)).

| Cited URL class (business absent) | Task template | Grade | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third-party "best X" listicle | Pitch the author for inclusion and a high position, with a differentiator and proof; mark lists updated in the last ~2 months as "live" | B | Paid placement: no evidence either way; must be disclosed |
| Review platform (G2, Capterra, Yelp, Tripadvisor, Healthgrades, Avvo, Houzz) | Claim and complete the profile; ask every customer for a review | B | FTC rule: no fake, AI-written or sentiment-conditioned reviews (~$51k per violation); no review gating ([WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials/)) |
| Directory/listing (GBP, Bing Places, Apple, MapQuest, Clutch, Crunchbase) | Create or fix the listing; match NAP to the site | B (local) | Choose directories the audit shows being cited, not bulk citation services |
| Reddit/Quora thread | Disclosed founder or staff reply that genuinely answers the question | C | Volatile in ChatGPT; steadier in Google surfaces and Perplexity; no astroturfing |
| youtube.com video (common in AI Overviews) | Explainer, how-to or comparison video with the brand in the title, description and transcript | B | Gemini and Copilot reportedly rarely cite YouTube |
| News/trade press | Pitch the outlet; plan monthly coverage | C | Citation half-life is 3.4 weeks in ChatGPT and 5.8 in Perplexity ([Scrunch](https://scrunch.com/blog/half-life-of-ai-citations)) |
| Wikipedia/Wikidata | Disclosed talk-page request or Wikidata fix, only if the business meets notability | C | Undisclosed paid editing breaks Wikipedia's terms |
| Competitor's own page | No outreach; build or improve your own honest comparison page | C | — |

Citations decay, so off-site tasks should recur. Re-pitching and refreshing every 4-6 weeks fits the measured half-lives better than one-time tasks. Vendor estimates put pickup at 1-7 days for Perplexity, 3-14 for ChatGPT search, and 2-3 weeks for AI Overviews after a strong placement (grade C, [AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/blog/earned-media-ai-citation-timeline-2026)).

## Wrong facts get fixed at the source; feedback buttons only create a record

AI-stated brand facts are wrong often enough to warrant their own module. In a study of 32,556 facts about 1,704 brands, each graded by someone at that brand, **9.2% were false**. Pricing had the highest error rate at 13.5% (16.1% in the US), followed by funding (10.9%) and integrations (10.6%). Small firms erred more often than large ones (9.4% vs 6.3%), AI/ML companies had an 18.5% error rate and SaaS 9.5%, and by engine Perplexity was at 11.5%, AI Overviews 11.4% and ChatGPT 8.1% (grade B, self-selected customers, [Searchable](https://www.searchable.com/data/ai-brand-misinformation-report)). For local businesses the most damaging 2026 pattern is a false "permanently closed". Anna Mae's Bakery in Ontario was reported as closing because Google's AI confused it with a same-named Illinois bakery, and Google blamed the "absence of high quality information on the web" ([PinMeTo](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/google-ai-overview-false-closure-location-accuracy-2026/)). Viridian Bookshop reported a significant sales drop and still saw the error weeks after filing Google's form ([Cleveland Magazine](https://clevelandmagazine.com/articles/willoughby-bookstore-confronts-google-ai-error-claiming-it-was-permanently-closed/)). Wrong phone numbers, including a scam number for United, have appeared in AI Overviews ([MarTech](https://martech.org/time-to-check-your-phone-numbers-in-ai-overviews)). Wolf River Electric is suing Google over an AI Overview that falsely said the state Attorney General was suing it ([Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2025/06/13/wolf-river-electric-google-ai-defamation-lawsuit/)).

No engine offers a business-facts correction process with a ticket or an outcome. Thumbs-down and feedback menus go to quality teams. OpenAI's Privacy Portal handles only *personal* data, which makes it relevant for a misdescribed founder but not for company facts, and OpenAI has offered to block outputs instead of correcting them ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001057); [Mezha](https://mezha.media/en/2024/04/29/openai-faces-a-complaint-in-the-eu-because-chatgpt-cannot-correct-false-information-about-people)). Claude's route is the same thumbs-down or feedback@anthropic.com ([Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8525154-claude-is-providing-incorrect-or-misleading-responses)). Every false-closure case found was resolved only after press coverage. The fix that works is changing the source, and how fast it lands depends on the type of edit. In MaxAEO's study of 312 edits, changing a fact on an already-cited page showed up in a median of **4 days**, a new Q&A section in 8, and a rewrite or new URL in 16. A claim contradicted by third-party sites took **33 days, and 41% never changed** within 45 days. Changing only the page date did nothing. Median pickup by engine was Perplexity 2 days, Claude 7, Copilot 8, AI Overviews 9, ChatGPT search 11 and Gemini 13, and 16% of edits never appeared at all (grade B, SaaS pages only, [MaxAEO](https://maxaeo.ai/blog/how-long-ai-updates-answers/)).

Each wrong-fact finding should be placed in one of five root-cause buckets, since the fix depends on the bucket. Those buckets are: a stale first-party page; a stale third-party listing or profile; model memory (no citation, and the error persists with search off); entity confusion with a same-named business; and gap-filling from category patterns, where no page states the fact either way.

| Wrong fact | Likely source | Fix steps | Expected time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price, plan or free tier (SaaS) | Old pricing URLs, G2/Capterra fields, listicles | Plain-text /pricing; 301 or noindex old URLs; FAQ line "Do you have a free plan?"; update review-site profiles; email the authors of cited lists | ~4-11 days; 30+ if third parties disagree |
| Feature or integration claimed but not offered | Category pattern-matching, competitor comparison pages | Explicit "what we do / don't do" and integrations pages; FAQ phrased like the triggering prompt | ~8 days median |
| Hours, phone, address | GBP, Bing Places, Apple, Yelp, Facebook, aggregators | Fix GBP first, then the rest; website NAP identical | Days |
| "Permanently closed" | Same-name confusion, stale listing | Confirm GBP shows open; "We're open" on site, GBP posts and social; feedback; GBP Help Community; press if revenue is hit; disambiguation | Weeks in documented cases |
| Founder or history | Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Wikidata, old press | Update those and the About page; OpenAI Privacy Portal for a named person | Weeks; memory takes months |
| Confused with a same-named company | Stronger entity dominates | About/FAQ line "Is [Brand] the same as [Other]? No..."; schema `@id`/`sameAs`; Wikidata item with location and founding date; "name + city" in listings ([Parse](https://www.parse.gl/blog/entity-disambiguation-ai)) | Weeks to months; no case study |
| Lawsuit, fraud or other legal claim | News about others mixed with your name | Timestamped evidence and a harm log; ask the cited publishers to clarify; legal letter | Unknown |

Verification should copy MaxAEO's rule. Re-run the exact triggering prompt three times per engine at days 3, 7, 14, 30 and 45. Count a fix as resolved when the correct fact appears in at least 2 of 3 runs on two consecutive checks. Test ChatGPT and Claude with search on and off: if the answer is right with search on and wrong with it off, the problem is model memory, and only the search-on answer can be fixed now. Legal action has not yet won anything. Walters v. OpenAI lost on summary judgment ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/georgia-court-dismisses-defamation-lawsuit-against-openai-over-chatgpt-output)), and Starbuck v. Google survived a motion to dismiss in July 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/esg/google-ordered-to-face-robby-starbucks-ai-defamation-lawsuit)). The product should only suggest it for false claims about crime, lawsuits, fraud, or a closure that is causing measurable loss.

## Rules that turn each audit field into a specific task

Taken together, the evidence supports a deterministic rule set. Each audit field triggers a task template filled in with the actual prompt, engine, competitor and URL, and each task carries a grade, a target metric and a re-check date. The local and SaaS task libraries differ mainly in which sources they target and which page types they ask for.

| Audit signal | Trigger | Task generated (filled with real values) | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website check: bot blocked or JS-only content | Any failure | "Allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt" / "Render prices on /pricing in HTML" (top priority: blocks every other task) | A/B |
| Website check: no explicit price, specs, comparison, or old date | Missing on a page that should answer a lost prompt | "Add 'Starting at $X' and a last-updated date to /services/botox" | A |
| Buyer question where the business wasn't named, and own site not cited | Lost prompt with no owned page that matches | Create or rewrite one page per lost prompt cluster, answer in the first third | B |
| AI search queries (fan-out) | Fan-out terms ("price", "reviews", "vs [competitor]", "2026", `site:brand.com`) with no matching page | Build the missing page type, but only for real unmet needs, not one page per phrase ([Superlines](https://superlines.io/articles/90-day-geo-plan-ai-search-visibility)) | B |
| Cited URLs where the business is absent | Weighted by prompts × engines citing it | Class-based off-site task from the URL table above, naming the domain or thread | B/C |
| Competitors named | Competitor appears on cited URL X | "Competitor Y is on [listicle]; pitch [author] for inclusion"; SaaS: build "[Brand] vs Y" page | B |
| Named but not cited | Mention without own-URL citation | Strengthen the owned page the engine should cite (Ahrefs: links in only 28% of answers that mention it, [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-citations-vs-impressions-study/)) | B |
| Wrong-fact finding | Any | Root-cause bucket → fix table above; feedback logged as "record only"; re-test schedule | B |
| Local business type | GBP incomplete, NAP mismatch, few reviews on cited review domains | GBP completion; NAP fixes on cited directories; review drive on the 2-3 review sites the engines cite for that vertical | B |

Priority follows the factors agencies use, combined into a score the user can see: **how often engines cite the source × how many engines and prompts are affected × competitor dominance × business value of the prompt, divided by effort**. Blockers (crawl access, render failures) and harmful wrong facts (closure, phone, legal) override the score. The order is local-first or SaaS-first depending on business type. For local businesses: fix blockers and wrong facts, then GBP and NAP on cited directories, then service × city pages with prices, then best-of lists and local press, then reviews on the cited review sites. For SaaS: blockers and wrong prices, then the pricing, comparison and integrations pages, then third-party listicles and press, then G2, Capterra and GetApp, then Reddit and YouTube where the audit shows those domains are cited.

## Conclusion

The research points to a reversal of how GEO is usually sold. Format tricks such as schema, llms.txt and rewriting for the LLM are marketed as the core work but have the weakest evidence, and some of them reduce retrieval. What actually moves answers is mostly outside the page or plainly factual: whether the engine can read the price, whether the cited listicle includes you, and whether third-party sources contradict your own pages. Because engines cite different sources, churn them within weeks, and answer from memory most of the time in ChatGPT, a one-time strategy document goes stale quickly. The more defensible product is a list that is regenerated each scan from fresh citation data, with recurring tasks and day-3-to-45 re-tests.

The biggest gap in the evidence is causality. No independent before-and-after study exists for any tactic, and no local-business case study with numbers was found. A product that records which tasks were completed and how the tracked prompts changed afterward would produce the first real outcome data in this category, across many businesses. That data would also be its strongest advantage, because competitors' recommendations rest on correlations and vendor estimates.
