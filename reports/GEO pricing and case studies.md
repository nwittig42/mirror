# Mirror belongs between trackers and agencies

Mirror today delivers more than a tracking tool and less than an agency, so it should be priced in the gap between them: **Watch at $195 a month and Guide at $395 a month**, both reached through the free first audit, both month to month with no setup fee. As of October 2026, software that only watches what AI says costs **$17 to $129 a month**, done-for-you packages aimed at local businesses start at **$499**, and mainstream agencies start at **$1,500 to $3,000 with three- to six-month minimums**. Mirror's draft ladder of $495 / $995 / $1,995 has two problems. Its top two tiers sell fixing, and the agents that will do the fixing are not built yet; what exists is the audit, weekly scans of five assistants, fact-sheet checking, competitor reporting and reports. And its $495 entry tier charges a done-for-you price for work the owner still has to do, to an audience where a third of businesses have under $1,000 a month for all marketing. Done-for-you fixing should appear on the price list as a later plan, clearly marked "not yet available", at a suggested **$495 to $795 a month** when it ships. The structure rests on reasonable evidence; the exact dollar figures are judgment, because no survey has asked small-business owners what they would pay for AI visibility or accuracy and no benchmark exists for turning a free audit into a paid plan. Proof that this work pays off is also thin for local businesses: there is **not one named, independently checkable case study** of a plumber, dentist, law firm, restaurant or gym, so Mirror's website should lean on evidence that AI gets business facts wrong, which is much better documented.

## Watching is now a $17 to $50 product, so Mirror cannot charge $495 for it

The cheapest credible tools a single-location business can buy are Rankscale at **$17 a month** (billed yearly) ([Rankscale](https://rankscale.ai/pricing)), Local Falcon at $24.99 ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/pricing)), Otterly at $29 ([Otterly](https://otterly.ai/pricing)), BrightLocal at **$41 with AI tracking included at no extra charge** ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/pricing/)), and HubSpot's standalone tracker at $50 ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/aeo-grader)). The usual "real" entry tier is $79 to $129 (LLMrefs $79, Writesonic $79 billed yearly, Promptwatch $95, SE Ranking $129), and mid tiers sit at $189 to $500 (Otterly $189, Scrunch $300, AthenaHQ $295, Goodie $399) ([LLMrefs](https://llmrefs.com/pricing), [Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/pricing), [Promptwatch](https://promptwatch.com/pricing), [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/subscription.html), [Scrunch](https://scrunch.com/pricing), [AthenaHQ](https://www.athenahq.ai/pricing), [Goodie](https://higoodie.com/pricing)). Nearly every vendor charges by the number of questions tracked, and a local business needs only 15 to 50 of them. Mirror's current $495 entry price is ten to twenty-five times the software floor.

Two details in the software market work in Mirror's favour. First, the cheap tiers rarely cover all five assistants. BrightLocal tracks ChatGPT and Google's AI products only ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/local-seo-tools/local-ai-visibility)); Otterly sells Claude as an add-on at $29 to $439 a month ([Otterly](https://otterly.ai/pricing)); Writesonic keeps Claude, Perplexity and Grok for enterprise customers ([Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/pricing)); and Peec dropped Grok in June 2026 and moved Perplexity to enterprise-only in September ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/peec)). Second, almost nobody checks facts. The only productised accuracy check found is **Profound's FactCheck**, launched July 14, 2026, which works the way Mirror does: it compares what AI says against a knowledge base the brand supplies and traces wrong claims to their source ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/newsroom/profound-launches-factcheck-to-measure-the-accuracy-of-ai-answers-about-your-brand)). But Profound now lists only a free trial and a custom enterprise plan for brands ([Profound pricing](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing)), so fact checking is in practice an enterprise-priced feature. None of the fourteen other pricing pages read listed an accuracy check, and no software vendor sells fixing as a service. That is "not found", not "does not exist": feature pages were not all read.

The direction of travel matters too. HubSpot, Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking and BrightLocal now bundle AI tracking into existing subscriptions or sell it for $50 to $100, while Profound, the best-funded leader, pulled its $99 and $399 self-serve plans in mid-September 2026, days after raising $180M, according to a competitor's write-up ([GEO Toolbox](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/profound-pricing)). Watching is turning into a commodity. The money is moving to doing the work.

A free first check is also standard, not special. HubSpot, Ahrefs and Search Atlas all give one away ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/aeo-grader), [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/free-ai-visibility), [Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/ai-visibility/)), and five of the six small-business-facing service sellers whose pages were opened offer a free audit. Those free checks score visibility or brand perception. None claims to compare AI answers with the business's real details, which is the one way Mirror's free audit differs.

## Done-for-you sellers split into a $499 to $1,997 local band and a $3,000 agency floor

Services fall into two separate markets, and Mirror belongs in the cheaper one.

The agency figures come from one dataset: First Page Sage's October 2026 report of 1,146 quotes from 214 agencies. First Page Sage sells this service itself and its method could not be checked, so treat the numbers as the best available rather than as settled. That report puts the overall retainer median at **$6,850 a month**, with legal at $8,170, healthcare at $7,960 and home services lowest at $3,190 ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/geo-aeo-pricing-statistics/)). Even Mirror's top draft price is below the agency floor, so "less than an agency's cheapest plan" holds at every tier.

Three things in the service market shape the recommendation. Accuracy is sold as an extra, not as the headline: the one seller leading with "fix what AI gets wrong" targets software companies at $5,000 to $12,000 a month ([DerivateX](https://derivatex.agency/use-cases/fix-ai-hallucinations-about-your-brand/), [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/geo-aeo-pricing-statistics/)), and the closest local competitor hides accuracy monitoring behind its $3,500 tier. Competitors split their tiers by volume (questions tracked, pages written); none found splits them by accuracy work. And month to month with no setup fee is competitive but already offered by at least two productised sellers, so it is a hygiene factor rather than a selling point.

Paid one-off audits cost $1,500 to $12,000 at agencies ([First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/geo-aeo-pricing-statistics/)), and one small-business seller labels its free audit "a $349 value" ([HireAWiz](https://www.hireawiz.com/aeo-audit/)). That is the only stated price for a small-business audit and a usable anchor for putting a dollar value on Mirror's free audit.

## A third of small businesses have under $1,000 a month for all marketing

The draft prices were set for med spas. The general audience is poorer and less convinced.

LocaliQ's survey of 730+ owners and marketers found **33% have a marketing budget under $1,000 a month**, 5% have none, and businesses with ten or fewer staff are 31% more likely to sit under $500. It also found 61% do not buy SEO at all and only 19% buy reputation management ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)). So Mirror is selling the newer idea to owners who mostly never bought the older one, and $495 would be the entire budget for many of the smallest.

What they do buy clusters in three bands. Tools and care plans cost under $100. Mass-market vendors charge $150 to $450: Yelp Ads from $150 ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/)), Thryv from $244 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/156926/Thryv/pricing/)), Birdeye at $299 to $449 ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye)) and Podium at $399 to $599 ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/compare/birdeye-vs-podium/)); all of those are third-party reported figures, not vendor list prices. Human-run retainers cost $500 to $2,000: in SE Ranking's survey of 260 agencies that mostly serve small and local businesses, **30% charge under $500 a month and 64% under $1,000** ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)). Mirror's draft has nothing in the middle band where owner-operators already spend, and its middle tier sits right at the level where most small-business retainers stop.

Whether the fee pays back depends on the trade, and the data here is weak. Vendor tables put an average HVAC job at $1,850 and a plumbing job at $950 ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/home-services-marketing-benchmarks-by-trade/)), which means $495 a month is covered in revenue terms by roughly three HVAC jobs or six plumbing jobs a year. That table has no stated source, margins were not researched, and nothing reliable was found for bakeries, restaurants, salons or legal case values. Mirror should not promise payback to low-ticket trades.

Demand is real but not urgent. On the customer side, BrightLocal found use of AI tools for local recommendations jumped **from 6% to 45% of US adults in a year** ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)), and Yext found 47% used AI for local search in the past month, with 58% then visiting the business website and 62% checking Google ([Yext](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)). On the owner side, a May 2026 Bluehost survey of 350 owners found **only 13% are acting on AI search**, 22% had never heard of it, yet 78% want a report showing how competitors appear in AI answers and 47% would make it a top priority if a competitor showed up first ([Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/state-of-small-business-ai-confidence)). An older survey of 803 owners found 48% think AI search will have no effect on them ([Digital Third Coast](https://www.digitalthirdcoast.com/blog/seo-llm-business-owner-survey)). The lesson for the free audit is specific: show the wrong facts, and show a named competitor.

Price and loyalty pull in opposite directions. Older ProfitWell data puts median monthly churn at 6 to 9% for subscriptions under $100 and 3 to 4% above $500 ([ProfitWell](https://profitwell.com/blog/average-revenue-churn-rate-benchmarks)); SE Ranking found the agencies that lose clients within six months all charge $1,000 or less ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)); and a higher share of prepaid annual contracts goes with lower churn at every price ([Paddle / ProfitWell](https://paddle.com/studios/shows/profitwell-report/largest-study-on-churn)). A cheaper door widens the audience and loosens the grip, which is why the plan below pairs lower prices with a yearly prepay option.

## The recommended plan: free audit, then Watch $195 or Guide $395, with fixing to come

The plan starts with the free audit, sells only what Mirror can deliver today, and shows fixing as a future plan. The structure follows from the evidence. The numbers are judgment and are labelled as such below.

### Where Mirror sits today

Mirror currently delivers three things a $17 to $129 tracker does not: it covers all five assistants, it checks answers against a fact sheet the owner approved instead of counting mentions, and it hands over a readable report with a fix list in priority order. It does not yet deliver what a $499-plus service does, which is the work itself. The price has to respect both edges. The floor is the top of the tracker entry band, about $130. The ceiling is $499, where ZeroGravity starts doing technical work for the customer; charging that much for a report and a to-do list invites a comparison Mirror loses. That leaves **roughly $150 to $450**, which is also the band where small businesses already pay Yelp, Thryv and Birdeye. The nearest like-for-like prices found are a self-serve audit tool with error detection and weekly re-scans at $199 ([Pixelmojo](https://www.pixelmojo.io/answers/how-much-does-an-ai-visibility-audit-cost)) and Otterly's mid tier at $189 ([Otterly](https://otterly.ai/pricing)).

### The plan

| Step | Price | What the customer gets | What moves them up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free audit** (available now) | $0, once | One scan of all five assistants, checked against a draft fact sheet the owner approves. A readable audit report showing every wrong fact found and how one named competitor appears. | Seeing at least one wrong fact, or the competitor recommended first |
| **Watch** (available now) | $195 a month | Weekly scans of five assistants, every answer checked against the approved fact sheet, an alert when a new wrong fact appears, one competitor tracked, a monthly report, and a prioritized fix list. One location. | Fix-list items still open after 30 days; a second location; wanting more competitors tracked |
| **Guide** (available now; the plan to steer people to) | $395 a month | Everything in Watch, plus step-by-step fix instructions written so the owner or their web person can act on them, a re-check that confirms each fix held, three competitors tracked, a 30-minute monthly review call, and up to three locations. | Owner has no time or no web person to do the fixes; four or more locations |
| **Fix** (not yet available) | Suggested $495 to $795 a month, plus a one-time cleanup fee of $295 to $495 | Everything in Guide, plus Mirror's agents correct wrong facts where they come from: the website, Google profile, Yelp, Facebook and directories. The monthly report lists each fix made. | Facts are clean but AI still recommends the competitor |
| **Grow** (not yet available) | Suggested $995 to $1,495 a month | Everything in Fix, plus work to get recommended: question-and-answer and comparison pages, structured data, a review routine. | — |

**Terms today.** Month to month, cancel anytime, no setup fee on either plan. Prepaying a year costs ten months (about 17% off), which matches the "two months free" and 17% annual discounts that Goodie, Scrunch and AthenaHQ publish ([Goodie](https://higoodie.com/pricing), [Scrunch](https://scrunch.com/pricing), [AthenaHQ](https://www.athenahq.ai/pricing)). Four or more locations are quoted individually. Customers on Watch or Guide get first access to Fix when it ships, with any unused prepaid months credited toward it; that gives early buyers a reason to start now and gives Mirror a waiting list that measures real demand for fixing.

### Why each choice was made

**The free audit comes first because the sale is partly education.** Only 13% of owners are acting on AI search and 78% want to see how competitors appear. A free check is standard in this market, so the audit has to do what the others do not: show the specific wrong facts and a named competitor. One seller labels its free audit "a $349 value" ([HireAWiz](https://www.hireawiz.com/aeo-audit/)); Mirror can do the same.

**Watch at $195, not $495.** A plan in which the owner still does the fixing cannot carry a done-for-you price. $195 is about four to ten times the cheapest trackers, which is defensible because no self-serve fact checker under $300 was found and the cheap tiers leave out Claude, Grok or Perplexity. It is $2,340 a year, covered in revenue terms by one or two HVAC jobs or about three plumbing jobs at the vendor ticket figures cited above. This price assumes scanning and reporting are automated; if a person spends more than about an hour a month on each Watch customer, it is too low.

**Guide at $395 is where human time is sold.** The step-by-step instructions, the re-check and the monthly call are deliverable now without agents, and they answer the main weakness of a monitoring product: a fix list nobody acts on. It stays under the $499 line on purpose. The monthly call is the cost to watch; if calls cannot be kept to half an hour, raise the price or make the call quarterly.

**No setup fee today.** The draft charges $495 for setup on the cheapest plan. With no fixing there is no heavy first-month work to pay for, and the fact sheet is already built during the free audit. A setup fee here only adds friction to a low-commitment first step.

**Fix is shown but not sold.** Listing it as "not yet available" tells buyers where the product is going without promising work Mirror cannot yet do. Until it ships, the website must not say Mirror fixes anything. The suggested $495 to $795 comes from the productised local sellers who do comparable work today: ZeroGravity at $499 to $699 and AI Search Rankings at $799. The cleanup fee belongs here, not on Watch, because correcting years of wrong listings is front-loaded and published timelines say corrections take 2 to 12 weeks to show up in AI answers ([DerivateX](https://derivatex.agency/use-cases/fix-ai-hallucinations-about-your-brand/)); a month-to-month customer could cancel after the expensive part and before the result is visible. There is precedent: ZeroGravity charges $199 on a $499 plan and Merchynt about $499 on Google profile management ([SlashPost](https://slashpost.ai/blogs/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-optimization-cost)). This range is the least certain number in the report. It depends on what agent fixing costs to run and how much a person still has to check, neither of which is known yet.

**"Grow" instead of "Dominate".** The name promises more than the evidence supports. ChatGPT recommended only 1.2% of the multi-location brand locations SOCi studied ([SOCi](http://www.soci.ai/news/in-ai-driven-discovery-few-brands-are-chosen-most-disappear/)), and no local case study shows a service reliably changing that.

**One price list for all trades, for now.** Legal and healthcare agency medians are more than double home services, so a single ladder leaves money on the table with law firms and dentists. That gap matters more once Fix and Grow exist. Trade-specific pricing is worth testing after the first fifty customers, not before.

The steelman for the draft prices deserves a hearing. Even $1,995 is below the agency floor, higher prices go with lower churn in every dataset found, and cost was the top concern for only 5% of owners in the Bluehost survey ([Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/state-of-small-business-ai-confidence)). Those points argue for higher prices once Mirror does the fixing. They do not rescue $495 for a report and a list.

### Three signs the pricing is wrong

These thresholds are judgment; no outside benchmark exists for the first two.

The first sign is the **audit-to-paid rate**. Track the first 50 to 100 free audits that find at least one wrong fact and lead to a conversation. If fewer than one in ten become paying within 30 days and price is the stated reason, the prices are too high. If more than four in ten say yes without pushing back, they are too low.

The second is the **plan mix after 90 days**. If fewer than one in five paying customers choose Guide, the extra $200 is not buying enough and Guide needs more in it or a lower price. If more than two in three choose Guide, Watch is underpriced or Guide is, and Guide can move toward $450.

The third is **monthly churn**. Watch losing more than 6% of customers a month, or Guide more than 4%, means the price is ahead of the value shown; the ProfitWell medians are 6 to 9% under $100 and 3 to 4% above $500, and Mirror sits between. Read churn alongside the share of fix-list items owners actually complete. If lists sit untouched and customers leave in months two and three, the problem is the missing fixing, not the price, and it is the strongest signal to ship Fix.

### Where the evidence is thin

No survey asks owners what they would pay for AI visibility or accuracy. No trustworthy benchmark exists for free-audit-to-paid conversion; published figures run from under 1% to 80% and are anecdotes. There is no churn data for local marketing retainers by trade, no evidence for specific price ceilings such as $500 or $1,000, and no like-for-like service to benchmark against: nothing found sells fact checking with a fix list to local businesses, and nothing found sells agent-run fixing at all. Most named-vendor prices for Birdeye, Podium and Thryv are third-party estimates. No owner or agency forum threads could be retrieved, so there is no anecdotal closed-deal pricing here. The placement between trackers and agencies rests on reasonable evidence. Every specific dollar figure, the future Fix and Grow ranges, the cleanup fee and the warning thresholds are informed guesses to be corrected by Mirror's first 50 to 100 audits.

## Proof exists for software companies and almost none for local businesses

About twenty traceable case studies exist. Nearly all concern software, fintech or online retail, and nearly all were published by the vendor that sold the tool. **No named, independently checkable case was found for a plumber, dentist, law firm, restaurant, salon or gym.** The nearest are an unnamed personal injury firm in an agency blog post and a nursing college with 24 campuses. Mirror's site should say this openly; producing even one named local case would set it apart, because competitors have none either.

### (a) Safe to cite, with the company named and the word "reported"

| Company | What it reported | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Vercel | ChatGPT referred about 10% of new signups, up from 1% six months earlier ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/how-were-adapting-seo-for-llms-and-ai-search)) | "Vercel reported in 2025…"; do not give a month, roundups date it inconsistently |
| Webflow | 10% of signups from AI discovery, growing 4x in a year ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/traffic-is-no-longer-reliable)) | Also found that an llms.txt file produced no real lift |
| Docebo | AI tools were the source for 12.7% of high-intent leads in Q3 2025, about five times a year earlier ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/ai-discovery-playbook)) | Measured with a "how did you hear about us" question |
| Ahrefs | AI search was 0.5% of traffic and 12.1% of signups ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-traffic-conversions-ahrefs/)) | One site, one month; the author doubts it will scale |
| Tally | ChatGPT became its top referral source ([Tally](https://blog.tally.so/from-2-to-3m-arr-how-we-bootstrapped-tally-with-a-tiny-team/)) | Shows AI can become a top channel. Tally ran no campaign, so it is not proof a service works |
| Arizona College of Nursing | 51% more AI-referred enrollment inquiries in 90 days across 24 local markets ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/customers/arizona-college-of-nursing)) | Vendor-published; say "according to Profound" |
| MongoDB | Over 90% accuracy in AI answers after flagging wrong citations and fixing them at the source ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/customers/mongodb)) | Closest public match to Mirror's method; vendor-published, no time period |
| Princeton-led research paper | Adding quotations, statistics and sources raised visibility in AI answers by up to about 40% in a lab test; smaller, lower-ranked sites gained most ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)) | A lab measure of share of an answer, not leads or sales |

All of these are large or online companies. They show the channel is real, not that it works for a bakery. Results also took time: visibility numbers moved within weeks in vendor cases, while leads and signups were reported over roughly 90 days to six months.

### (b) Evidence on how often AI gets business facts wrong

This is the strongest ground for Mirror, though every study comes from a company that sells a related product. SOCi's 2026 index of 350,000+ locations found ChatGPT, Perplexity and other non-Google assistants are **only about 68% accurate on business contact details**, with Gemini much better because it draws on Google Maps ([SOCi](https://golocal.soci.ai/rs/355-TUF-572/images/The_2026_Local_Visibility_Index_SOCi100.pdf?version=0)). Searchable put 72,000+ questions about UK high street retailers to three assistants and found **64% of businesses had at least one false fact returned**, one answer in sixteen was false, and smaller businesses were hit harder ([Retail Focus](https://retail-focus.co.uk/nearly-two-thirds-of-uk-high-street-retailers-suffer-from-ai-chatbot-inaccuracies/)). A second Searchable study found 93% of 165 London businesses had a basic fact wrong or missing ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-answers-about-your-locations-are-often-wrong-check-before-customers-do/582205/)). WHOOP found 7.9% of AI answers about it had accuracy problems ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/customers/whoop)).

Two framings are both true and must not be blurred. Per answer, error rates are modest, at 4 to 10% depending on the assistant. Per business, most end up with at least one wrong fact because each has many facts. The UK studies are not US data, and Searchable's retailer report does not say how many retailers were tested. SOCi also found AI draws local facts mainly from Google Maps (32.5%), the business's own website (23.1%), Yelp (10.5%) and Facebook (7.6%), which supports correcting those sources as the right lever.

No study measures revenue lost to wrong AI facts. The one dollar figure is a lawsuit: Minnesota solar installer Wolf River Electric alleges a false Google AI statement cost it customers with contracts worth up to $150,000 ([Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2025/06/13/wolf-river-electric-google-ai-defamation-lawsuit/)). It is an unproven allegation about reputation, not about wrong hours or prices, and should be described that way if used at all.

### (c) Claims not to cite, or to cite only with the counter-evidence

**Do not cite.** The widely repeated "Chicago personal injury firm: 156 clients and $2.34M from AI" does not appear on the source page it is attributed to, and its own numbers do not add up ([Intercore](https://intercore.net/llm-seo/case-study-personal-injury-law/), [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/answer-engine-optimization-case-studies)). The anonymous law firm claim of 12% of calls from ChatGPT gives no tracking method ([ROI Amplified](https://roiamplified.com/insights/show-up-on-chatgpt/)). Semrush's "AI visitors are worth 4.4x" has no sample size or method ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/)). Ramp's "7x in a month" conflicts with the results table on the same page ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/customers/ramp)). Go Fish Digital's "3x leads" headline sits on an 83% lift with no absolute numbers ([Go Fish Digital](https://gofishdigital.com/blog/generative-engine-optimization-geo-case-study/)). The Discovered Labs case is described as 4x, 6x and 600% in one page ([Discovered Labs](https://discoveredlabs.com/case-studies/b2b-saas-4x-ai-referred-trials-aeo-strategy)).

**Contested.** The claim that AI visitors convert far better than search visitors rests on single sites: Ahrefs at 23x, one Seer client at 15.9% against 1.76% ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/case-study-6-learnings-about-how-traffic-from-chatgpt-converts)). Larger studies disagree. Amsive's 54-site study found **no statistically significant difference** (4.87% against 4.60%) ([Amsive](https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/llm-traffic-conversion-rate-study/)); a 973-site academic working paper found ChatGPT referrals converting worse than Google, as reported by Search Engine Land ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/llms-google-referral-conversion-study-463747)); and Adobe found AI visitors to US retail sites 9% less likely to buy ([Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/03/17/adobe-analytics-traffic-to-us-retail-websites-from-generative-ai-sources-jumps-1200-percent)). The honest line is that AI referral traffic is small, growing fast, and converts very well for some companies and about the same as search across larger samples.

**Usable only with the caveat attached.** SOCi's "ChatGPT recommends 1.2% of locations" covers multi-location brands and counts only the top five of ten results. Local Falcon's "83% of restaurants never appear in ChatGPT" covers restaurants tracked by Local Falcon for one month ([Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/research/the-ai-visibility-crisis-why-83-percent-of-restaurants-dont-exist-in-chatgpt)). The "40% visibility boost" from the research paper is a lab result, and top-ranked sources lost visibility under one method. BrightLocal's 45% covers all AI tools, not ChatGPT alone.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question Mirror is answering. The draft asked "what is this worth compared with an agency?" and the answer was a comfortable $495 to $1,995. Two facts turn that into a different question. The audience is now every small business, whose owners already pay $150 to $450 a month without a long sales call. And the product today finds problems but does not yet solve them. The honest price for that is in the gap between trackers and agencies, which is also a gap nobody else is filling with fact checking.

That gap is temporary on both sides. Tracking is being bundled into $41 subscriptions, and Profound has shown that fact checking against a client's own source of truth can be turned into a product. What Mirror sells today will be worth less each year; the fixing agents are what hold a price. Until they ship, the Watch and Guide customers do two jobs beyond paying. Their conversion and churn numbers replace the guesses in this report, and their fix lists, done or left undone, show how much demand for fixing exists and supply the first named, measured local-business result that no competitor has. Asking each client's new customers "how did you hear about us" from day one is worth more than getting the first price exactly right, since ordinary analytics undercount AI referrals.
