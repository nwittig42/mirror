## 1. Target customer

### The ICP (in priority order)

**Primary: the owner-injector.** A nurse practitioner, PA, or MD who owns a 2–8 provider med spa or cosmetic derm practice doing roughly $800K–$3M/yr. Their name IS the brand ("Aesthetics by Dr. Kim"). They already spend $2–8K/mo on marketing, run their own Instagram, and personally feel every review. In LA specifically: Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Studio City, Pasadena, Manhattan Beach, Brentwood, dense competitive pockets where "best med spa in [neighborhood]" is a contested prompt.

**Secondary: the cosmetic dermatologist / plastic surgeon** with an established practice and a practice manager who handles vendors. Longer sales cycle, bigger budget, more credential-sensitive (board certification is their moat, an AI misstating it is a visceral pain).

**Tertiary (volume play later): multi-location med spa groups** (3+ locations), per-location pricing, sold to a marketing director.

### Buying triggers (lead with these, they're the "why now")
- They just Googled themselves and saw an AI Overview, or a patient said "ChatGPT sent me" / "ChatGPT said you don't do X."
- A competitor opened nearby or is beating them on Instagram.
- They just fired or are doubting an SEO agency ("I pay $2K/mo and I can't tell what they do").
- New location, new provider, or rebrand, exactly when AI info goes stale.

### Disqualifiers (don't waste pitches)
- Celebrity practices (already cited everywhere; no pain).
- Practices with <50 Google reviews and no site (nothing for AI to find; they need basics first, refer them out, collect goodwill).
- Groupon-driven discount spas (price-shoppers don't buy $995 retainers).

## 2. What they get out of it

Translate everything into their three currencies:

| Their currency | What we deliver |
|---|---|
| **New patients** | Show up in the AI shortlist when someone asks "best med spa in [neighborhood] for [treatment]", the highest-intent question a patient can ask. One retained filler patient = $15–25K lifetime value; the service pays for itself at one patient per year. |
| **Reputation control** | Weekly monitoring of what 4 AI engines literally tell patients about them, verbatim. They see the words. Nobody else shows them the words. |
| **Liability protection** | Catch AI stating wrong credentials, wrong pricing, fabricated services, before a patient or a regulator does. Courts have held AI companies not liable for false statements about you (Walters v. OpenAI, 2025). Nobody is accountable for this except the practice itself. |

## 3. The pitch (positioning)

**Spiky statement:**
> For LA aesthetic practices whose reputation is their name, we're an AI-visibility service that monitors and corrects what ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI tell patients about you. Unlike SEO agencies optimizing for a search page patients are abandoning, we manage the AI answers they actually read.

**Named enemy: the unsupervised AI answer.** Not Google, not other agencies, the fact that four AI engines are describing their practice to patients every day, with zero accountability, and nobody on their payroll has ever read what those answers say. (Secondary enemy when talking to agency-burned owners: the $2K/mo SEO retainer that can't answer "what does ChatGPT say about me?")

**One-line POV:** *"Your next patient isn't Googling you. She's asking ChatGPT. Nobody is checking its answer."*

**Proprietary phrase:** run everything under one named method, **the AI Accuracy Loop™** (Monitor → Diagnose → Fix → Prove, weekly). Clients repeat named things; they can't repeat a feature list.

**Three headline variants (for site/deck/outreach):**
1. "Know what AI tells your patients. Fix what it gets wrong."
2. "ChatGPT is recommending someone in [neighborhood]. Is it you?"
3. "The first AI-accuracy service for aesthetic medicine."

**Weakest Hormozi lever and the attack:** *Perceived likelihood of achievement*. You have no case studies and a skeptical, been-burned-by-agencies buyer. Attack: never sell with market data alone; sell with **their own data**. The free baseline audit converts "will this work?" into "this is already happening to me." Their own hallucination screenshot beats any case study you'll ever have. Support with the risk-reversal ("no material findings in the audit = no setup fee") and founding-client rate lock.

## 5. How we find them

1. **The audit-first list build (core motion).** Each week: pick one LA neighborhood → pull the top ~15 practices from Google Maps + Yelp ("med spa," "botox," "cosmetic dermatologist") → run the 5-minute screen (do they appear in any AI answer for 3 category prompts? any obvious inaccuracy on the branded prompt?) → the 3 with the worst gap-to-quality ratio (good reviews, invisible or misdescribed in AI) get full audits and pitches. 3 audits/week ≈ 12 pitches/mo.
2. **Instagram DM as the channel.** Med spa owners live on IG and answer DMs faster than email. Short version of Angle 1 + one screenshot. Email the practice manager as the formal follow-up.
3. **Referral loop from day one:** every "no" gets "who do you know who'd want to see their audit?"; every client gets one free month per referred client. Aesthetic owners all know each other (AmSpa events, injector trainings, reps).
4. **AmSpa / The Aesthetic Show / MedSpa associations**: one talk or booth ("What AI tells patients about your med spa, live audits") once you have 2–3 clients.
5. **Content flywheel (fits Crosspost later):** publish one "State of AI visibility: [neighborhood] med spas" mini-report monthly, leaderboard format. Practices share things they rank well in; the ones who rank poorly call you.

## 6. How we sell ourselves

- **The free baseline audit is the entire top of funnel.** Never pitch the service; pitch the findings. "I ran what AI says about you, 15 minutes to walk you through it?" is a meeting about *them*, not about buying something.
- **Sell the score** (Doc 3): "You're a 34/100. [Competitor] is a 61." A number they can't unknow.
- **Demo the platform live**: the Command Center with their brand already loaded (Watch-tier setup takes you an hour; do it before the call for hot prospects). Software they can log into makes a $995 service feel like an operating system, not a consultant.
- **Objection handling:**
  - *"My SEO agency handles this"* → "Ask them your visibility score on ChatGPT. It's a different channel with different rules, 89% of what Perplexity cites isn't what ChatGPT cites, and none of it is Google's top 10. We coexist with your SEO; we cover the four engines they don't."
  - *"Is anyone really finding med spas on ChatGPT?"* → don't argue stats; show their competitor being recommended in the live answer. "Someone is being chosen from this answer. It isn't you."
  - *"Can't I just check ChatGPT myself?"* → "Once, yes. We run 40 checks a week across four engines, catch changes within days, and fix the sources. It's a monitoring system, not a search."
- **Founding-5 offer** (from the pricing doc): lifetime rate lock + named case-study participation. Scarcity that's true.

## 7. Kill criteria for this positioning (60 days / 10 pitches)

1. If prospects keep responding "doesn't my SEO agency do this?" after hearing the pitch, the category framing failed, reposition harder on the accuracy/compliance wedge (drop "GEO/SEO" vocabulary entirely; lead with "AI accuracy audit").
2. If fewer than ~30% of pitched owners accept the premise that patients use AI to research providers, even after seeing the live answer, the trend urgency isn't real in this vertical yet; pivot the wedge from "win new patients" to pure "reputation/liability monitoring" (Watch tier as the lead product) and revisit growth positioning in two quarters.
