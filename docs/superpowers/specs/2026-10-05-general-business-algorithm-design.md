# General small-business algorithm: design

Date: 2026-10-05
Status: approved after a three-lens adversarial review; on branch `feat/general-business-algorithm`
Origin: the measurement techniques come from TrueSource (`alexdg832/geo-audit-mvp`), a sibling GEO audit tool. Every rule below is written as a decision procedure with named test inputs, because the first draft gave rules by example and three independent reviewers found inputs where each example-based rule produced the wrong number.

## Goal

Make the scan measure any small business (a plumber, a bakery, a law firm, a gym, a med spa) instead of only aesthetic practices, and make the inputs to the score more accurate, without changing the published score formulas. What changes is how reliably the things those numbers are built from are measured, and the words the product uses to talk about them.

This spec supersedes the vocabulary lock in `docs/plans/2026-07-28-geo-monitoring-mvp.md` ("practice", "patients", "providers", "treatments"). The two code comments that cite that lock (`src/app/page.tsx`, `src/app/book/page.tsx`) are updated to point here.

## Rules kept (Nick's locked decisions)

- Visibility = 50% named rate + 20% rank + 15% tool reach, rescaled over 0.85; never named on an earned prompt = 0.
- Fact score = 100 minus 25 / 12 / 4 per open critical / major / minor wrong fact, price errors x1.5; null when nothing was judged.
- Overall = 70% visibility + 30% facts; visibility 0 = 0; a critical wrong fact caps it at 70.
- Branded prompts never count toward visibility.
- A scan under 75% coverage is blocked, never published.
- Scan Thursday, Pulse Friday, with the gap between them.
- No em dashes in user-facing copy. No model id outside `src/lib/env.ts`.
- Database identifiers stay as they are (`practices`, `practice_id`, `practiceName`, `/admin/practices/[id]`, `/dashboard/[slug]`). Only words a person reads change. Schema changes are applied with `npm run db:push`, like every change since audit v2; the `drizzle/` migration folder has not been regenerated since then and is left alone, so this branch does not bundle that unrelated drift into a new migration file.

## What to expect on the first scan after this ships

The named rate moves the first time an existing client is scanned: tolerant matching adds mentions that exact matching missed, and echo detection, the longer-name guard and the namesake guard remove mentions that were never real. The operator should expect a one-week step in the trend and be able to explain it on the call; `docs/handoff.md` and the README say so. No formula changed.

## What changes

### 1. Name matching, with echo detection (`src/core/mention.ts`, `src/core/position.ts`)

Problems today. `detectMention` is an exact, word-bounded match of each variation. It misses "Harbor Plumbing" when the client typed "Harbor Plumbing LLC", "Joes Pizza" for "Joe's Pizza", "Skin Lab" for "SkinLab", and "Rusty Anchor" for "The Rusty Anchor". It counts "I couldn't find any information about Harbor Plumbing" as a mention. And because `mentioned` (scan runner) and `position` (`classifyPosition`) come from different functions, a check can be stored as named with position absent.

Matcher. A variation is compiled into a matcher as follows.
1. NFC-normalise and lower-case it. Split into tokens on whitespace, on punctuation, and on a lower-to-upper case boundary inside a word ("SkinLab" gives `skin`, `lab`; "NAKEDMD" has no such boundary and stays one token; "iPhone" gives `i`, `phone`). Delete apostrophes (`'`, `’`) inside a token ("joe's" gives `joes`). `&`, `+` and the word `and` all become the conjunction token `and`.
2. Distinctive tokens are the tokens that are not an article (the, a, an), not the conjunction, and not a legal suffix (llc, l.l.c, inc, incorporated, ltd, limited, co, corp, corporation, pllc, pc, p.c, dds, d.d.s, md, m.d, pa, p.a, lp, llp, gmbh, plc).
3. Trailing legal-suffix and conjunction tokens are dropped only while at least two distinctive tokens remain. "Harbor Plumbing LLC" becomes `harbor plumbing`; "The Pasta Co" keeps `co` (only `pasta` would remain), so it never matches "best place for pasta"; "Ava MD" and "Kim MD" keep `md`; "Crumb & Co" keeps `co`.
4. A leading article is optional only when at least two other non-conjunction tokens follow it ("The Rusty Anchor" matches "Rusty Anchor"); otherwise it is required, so "The Plumber", "The Spa" and "A&W" never match the bare word. No other token is optional except the conjunction (step 5): generic words such as group, center, family, associates, dental are required, so "Main Street Dental Group" does not match "Main Street Dental Associates".
5. Each token matches its own letters with an optional apostrophe allowed between any two letters and an optional possessive (`'s`, `’s`) after it, so `joes` matches "Joe's", "Joes" and "Joes'". A letter that decomposes matches both its composed form and its base letter followed by combining marks, so an NFD answer matches an NFC variation. The conjunction token matches `and`, `&` or `+` and is itself optional, so "Crumb & Crust Bakery" matches "Crumb and Crust Bakery" and "Crumb Crust Bakery". Between tokens, zero to four non-alphanumeric characters are allowed, never `!`, `?` or a line break, and a period only after a token of three letters or fewer ("Dr. Kim", "St. Mary's", "Mt. Hood"), so "SkinLab", "Skin Lab", "Skin-Lab" and "Skin & Lab" are one name while "serve Portland. Plumbing rates" is not "Portland Plumbing".
6. The whole pattern is bounded on both sides by a Unicode letter, digit or combining-mark boundary, case-insensitive.
7. A variation with no distinctive token (for example "LLC" or "The") never matches.

Longer-name guard. A match that is immediately followed, after zero to four non-alphanumeric characters that are not a comma, semicolon, period, `!`, `?`, line break or en/em dash, by `&`, `+` or the word `and`, then whitespace, then a capital letter, may be the first words of a longer business name ("Harbor Plumbing & Heating", "Glow and Grace Aesthetics"). It is rejected unless the capitalised word starts another known name, in which case the sentence is a list of businesses ("Skin Bar LA and Glow MedSpa are both good"), or the longer text itself matches another variation of the same business, or the word is `I` or a well-known platform (Yelp, Google, Google Maps, Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, Angi, Thumbtack, Houzz, Nextdoor, TripAdvisor, BBB, HomeAdvisor, Amazon, Reddit, YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Bing, Apple Maps, Yellow Pages). "Known names" are the names the caller passes as `others`: `classifyPosition` gives each side the other (the competitor list when matching the practice, the practice's variations when matching competitors), and the scan runner runs the competitor extractor before the pre-filter so a business the answer just discovered counts as known. A comma or a sentence end before the conjunction always marks a list or a new sentence. The guard applies to practice variations and competitor names alike.

Echo detection (practice variations only, never competitor names). A match is an echo when, inside the same sentence, a no-knowledge phrase precedes it and is linked to it by a preposition, and nothing marks the phrase as being about one attribute or the clause as being about the business:
- No-knowledge phrases: `could not find`, `couldn't find`, `can't find`, `cannot find`, `unable to find`, `wasn't able to find`, `was not able to find`, `didn't find`, `did not find`, `don't have`, `do not have`, `doesn't have`, `does not have`, `not aware of`, `not familiar with`, `no information`, `no details`, `no record`, `no reliable information`, `no specific information`, `little information`, `limited information`.
- Link: the phrase ends at most 60 characters before the match, and the text between them is optional filler words, then exactly one link word (`about`, `on`, `for`, `regarding`, `of`) directly after them, then optional filler words, then only punctuation, quotes or whitespace before the name. Fillers: a, an, the, this, that, any, much, more, many, some, specific, detailed, reliable, public, verified, available, current, information, details, data, review, reviews, record, records, result, results, listing, listings, anything, something, else, or, customer, online, recent, business, company, called, named. A phrase that carries its own preposition (`not aware of`, `not familiar with`) links directly to a name that follows it. So "no record of complaints for Harbor Plumbing" and "don't have time to shop around, go for Harbor Plumbing" are not echoes, while "couldn't find any information about Harbor Plumbing" and "no details on Harbor Plumbing" are.
- A contrast word (`but`, `however`, `that said`, `although`, `though`) between the phrase and the name breaks the link.
- Not an echo when the match is followed by a possessive and a word ("Harbor Plumbing's hours"), or when the first contrast word after the match introduces a clause that does not start with another known name: "..., but they are well reviewed" and "..., but they are cheaper than Roto-Rooter" keep the mention; "..., but Roto-Rooter is a solid option" stays an echo.
- Label form: a line "Harbor Plumbing: ..." is an echo only when the text after the colon starts with a no-knowledge phrase, after an optional lead-in (I'm, I, we, sorry, unfortunately, I have, there is, there's). "Harbor Plumbing: well reviewed on Yelp; no details on pricing" is a mention.
- Sentences are split on `.`, `!` or `?` followed by whitespace, on newlines, and at list markers (`-`, `*`, `•`, `1.`, `1)`).
- Kept as mentions: "I couldn't be happier with Harbor Plumbing" (no link), "Harbor Plumbing has no long-term contracts, and its reviews are excellent" (phrase after the name), "I couldn't verify whether Harbor Plumbing is licensed" (no link word), "I couldn't find information about Harbor Plumbing's pricing" (possessive), "Not much information on Harbor Plumbing, but they are well reviewed" (contrast clause about the business). Echoes: "I couldn't find any information about Harbor Plumbing", "I don't have details on Harbor Plumbing", "Harbor Plumbing: I'm not aware of this business", "I couldn't find any information about Harbor Plumbing, but Roto-Rooter and Mr. Rooter are solid options".

API. `findMatches(answer, names, others?)` returns every variation's first non-rejected match as `{ name, start, end }` (guard applied, no echo filtering) and is what the scan runner uses for discovered competitor names. `findMentions(answer, practiceNames, others?)` is the same with echo filtering. `findAllMatches` and `findAllMentions` return every accepted occurrence instead of the first, and are what `classifyPosition` uses so that a later real mention survives when the first occurrence is swallowed by a longer competitor name ("Rose City Plumbing Pros is a chain; Rose City Plumbing is the local one"). `detectMention(answer, practiceNames, others?)` is `findMentions(...).length > 0`. `mentionKind(answer, practiceNames, others?)` returns `"named"` (a non-echo match), `"echo"` (matches, all echoes) or `"absent"`. `detectNames` and `extractSnippet` take the same optional `others` and use `findMentions` semantics; every caller that holds a check row passes that row's competitors, so a mention the scan counted in a list sentence gets its snippet and highlight. `highlightRanges` keeps every occurrence including echoes: highlighting is for reading an answer. `nameTokens(name)` exposes the matcher's normalised tokens for the competitor reconciliation below.

One analysis drives everything. In the scan runner's record phase, `mentioned` is `position !== "absent"` from `classifyPosition`, so the containment suppression in `position.ts` (a longer competitor span swallowing the practice's) also clears `mentioned`. The network phase runs the competitor extractor first and then uses `mentionKind(answer, practiceNames, known and discovered competitors) === "named"` as its cheap pre-filter for the judge and the rater; a result the record phase then finds absent is discarded (see section 2 for what "discarded" means). `shouldJudge` for a branded prompt becomes `kind === "branded" && mentionKind(answer, practiceNames) !== "echo"`: a branded answer that only echoes the name has nothing to check, is not counted as judged, and so cannot push the fact score toward 100.

Competitor reconciliation (`src/core/competitors.ts`). Today a discovered name that contains a practice name is dropped, so "Harbor Plumbing & Heating" could never become a competitor. New rule, applied through the matcher's own tokens (`nameTokens`, with a trailing possessive stripped first): a discovered name is dropped when a practice key contains its key ("Glow" for "Glow MedSpa"), when its tokens equal a practice variation's tokens, or when its tokens start with a practice variation's tokens and the next token is not the conjunction (a location or descriptor tail, which the extractor was told to strip anyway). "Harbor Plumbing, Inc.", "Harbor Plumbing, LLC", "Harbor Plumbing's", "Harbor Plumbing, Portland" and "Harbor Plumbing-Portland" are all the practice; "Harbor Plumbing & Heating" is a different business and is kept. Known platforms (section 3) are dropped by exact key equality only.

Tests (`tests/core/mention.test.ts`, `tests/core/position.test.ts`, `tests/core/competitors.test.ts`, `tests/services/scan-runner.test.ts`). Every input named above, plus the existing cases, plus: "glowing" never matches "Glow"; "Main Street Dental Group" vs "Main Street Dental Associates"; "Harbor Plumbing & Heating is top rated" with client "Harbor Plumbing" gives `mentioned=false`, `position="absent"` whether or not the longer name is a known competitor; a branded echo answer with a fact sheet leaves `factScore` null and the judge uncalled; an echo in the same sentence as two competitor names keeps both competitors in `namedOrder`.

### 2. Namesake guard (`src/services/tone-rater.ts`, `src/core/tone.ts`, `src/services/scan-runner.ts`, admin answers view, `src/report/build-report.ts`)

Problem. Small-business names collide. An answer recommending the Denver "Harbor Plumbing" counts as a mention of the Portland client and gets fact-checked against the Portland fact sheet.

Design.
- The tone rater already runs on every answer that names the business. It is given the business's category, location and website when the practice row has them, and returns two more fields: `same_business` (`true`, `false`, or `null` when it cannot tell) and `evidence` (when `false`: the verbatim fragment of the answer that places the business somewhere else or in another trade, for example "in Denver, CO" or "a commercial HVAC contractor"). `parseToneOutput` accepts both as optional; a fake rater in a test that omits them yields `same_business: null`.
- The veto fires only when all of these hold: the practice has a non-empty `location`; the rater returned `false`; the evidence fragment, whitespace-normalised and case-insensitive, is found in the answer; and the sentence containing the practice's name does not contain the practice's city (the part of `location` before the first comma). Otherwise the rater's opinion is ignored and nothing changes.
- A vetoed check keeps the record phase's single `classifyPosition` result and overrides only the practice: `mentioned=false`, `position="absent"`, `competitorsMentioned` unchanged, `namedOrder` with the practice's canonical entry removed (the matcher is never re-run without the practice names, so a competitor joined to the namesake by "and" stays on the row), `tone=null`, `toneReason="Different business: <evidence>"`, `factsConfirmed=[]`; its judge result is discarded (not counted as judged, no findings). The guard's own occurrence search is given the same competitor list as `classifyPosition` (`others`), so "<namesake> and <known competitor> both serve Denver, CO" is evaluated against the four conditions instead of being skipped. An internal activity line records `namesake: <engine> described a different <name> on prompt <text> (<evidence>)`.
- "Discarded" (also used by section 1 for an earned-prompt answer the record phase finds absent): not counted in `judgedCheckCount`, no findings opened, `factsConfirmed=[]`, `tone=null`.
- The opposite gap is logged, never hidden: when a check on an earned prompt is stored as named but the pre-filter had skipped the judge (fact sheet present, no verdict) or the rater (rater configured, no tone), typically because the competitor extractor failed for that answer, one internal scan warning names the engine and prompt so the operator can see what was not fact-checked or rated.
- The admin answers tab (`src/app/admin/practices/[id]/overview/sections.tsx`) shows a "Different business" pill with the evidence on such a check. `build-report.ts` never picks a vetoed check as the "AI recommended others and not you" quote (`missedQuote`), because its text names the client's name.
- The admin "Add a business" form gains optional category and location inputs (same 60 / 80 caps as `updateReportDetails`), so a business the operator creates by hand has a location on record from the start. `createPractice` takes them.

Tests. Rater parse with and without the fields. Scan runner: veto fires on a practice with a location when the evidence is in the answer and the sentence lacks the city (wrong fact kept out of `findings`, right facts kept out of `factsConfirmed`, `judgedCheckCount` unchanged); no veto when the practice has no location; no veto when the evidence is not in the answer; no veto when the sentence names the city; a rater that throws changes nothing.

### 3. Competitor extraction for any trade (`src/services/competitor-extractor.ts`, `src/core/competitors.ts`)

- The extractor prompt names what it returns without a vertical bias: "business, company, firm, shop, studio, restaurant, contractor, practice, clinic or provider", and keeps every business the answer recommends, including franchise and chain locations (Roto-Rooter, Mr. Rooter, Jiffy Lube, Molly Maid are competitors when recommended). It excludes: directories, review sites, marketplaces and apps (Yelp, Google Maps, Angi, Thumbtack, Houzz, Nextdoor, TripAdvisor, OpenTable, DoorDash, Zillow, Avvo, RealSelf, Groupon, Amazon); a product or parent brand named only to describe something else ("a Toyota dealer" names Toyota as a product, "Botox" is a product); generic categories ("a local plumber"); people who are not businesses.
- `reconcileCompetitors` drops a discovered name whose exact key is a known platform: yelp, google, google maps, google business profile, bing, bing places, apple maps, yellow pages, yp, angi, angie's list, homeadvisor, thumbtack, houzz, nextdoor, facebook, instagram, tiktok, youtube, linkedin, reddit, tripadvisor, bbb, better business bureau, groupon, doordash, uber eats, grubhub, opentable, resy, zillow, realtor.com, amazon, realself, healthgrades, zocdoc, vitals, webmd, avvo, findlaw, justia, expertise.com, three best rated. Exact key equality only: "Amazon Cleaning Co" survives.

Tests. Extractor prompt test with a Roto-Rooter sentence expecting all names; reconcile tests for the platform list and the conjunction-tail rule.

### 4. Questions for any trade, with a deterministic fallback (`src/services/question-drafter.ts`, `src/core/prompts.ts`, `src/services/prospect-setup.ts`)

- `draftQuestionsFromPages` takes optional `category` and `location`. When given, the prompt states them as fact ("The business is a <category> in <location>") and the model uses them; it still returns its own guess for whatever was blank. The prompt shows examples from three trades (a plumber, a bakery, a med spa). Among the five category questions it requires one with the city, one asking for the best option for a specific service the site offers, and one comparison ("who should I go to for X in <city>"). No "near me" phrasing anywhere: the engine adapters send no user location, so "near me" cannot be answered with the client's city. The 5 / 3 / 2 split, the kinds and the output shape are unchanged.
- `src/core/prompts.ts` (new, pure): `generateFallbackPrompts({ name, category, location })` returns a fixed, ordered set of six questions, every one naming the location: four category ("What are the best <plural> in <location>? Name specific businesses.", "Can you recommend a good <category> in <location>?", "I'm looking for a <category> in <location>. Which ones should I consider?", "Compare the top <plural> in <location>. Which would you choose?") and two branded ("What do you know about <name> in <location>?", "What are people saying about <name> in <location>?"). Deterministic for the same input; `pluralize` handles s/x/z/ch/sh and consonant-y endings.
- `prepareProspect`: the lead's category and location are authoritative when given and are passed to the drafter; the drafter's guesses fill only blanks. When the drafter returns no questions and both a category and a location are known, the fallback set is inserted and an activity line says so; the lead is scanned. When either is missing, today's behaviour stands: the lead is parked as "needs a look" and nothing is scanned or emailed.
- `prepareProspect` never leaves a lead in "preparing": the practice insert retries with the next slug suffix on a unique-slug collision (two same-name leads prepared at once get `<slug>` and `<slug>-2`), and any other thrown error ends the setup with status "needs a look" and a note carrying the error message.
- The fact drafter's category guidance describes people as "named people who deliver the service or run the business (owner, lead technician, chef, attorney, stylist, injector), with titles and credentials as written" and gives examples from several trades. Enum values are unchanged.

Tests (`tests/core/prompts.test.ts`, `tests/services/prospect-setup.test.ts`, `tests/services/question-drafter.test.ts`): determinism and city in every prompt; drafter receives the lead's category and location; fallback used when the drafter returns nothing and both are known; category known but no location: no fallback, no scan, `needs_attention`, `sendAudit` not called.

### 7. Two more website checks, and one made trade-neutral (`src/core/website-check.ts`, `src/services/website-check.ts`, `src/report/types.ts`, `src/report/build-report.ts`, `src/report/pages/master-website.tsx`, `docs/audit-design/HANDOFF.md`)

- `business_schema`, label "Your business is labeled for machines". `jsonLdNodes(html)` returns the parsed JSON-LD nodes (`jsonLdTypes` stays). A business node is one whose `@type` is `LocalBusiness`, `Organization` or any type in the ported schema.org business-type list, or matches /Business|Organization|Store|Shop|Service|Restaurant|Dentist|Clinic|Salon|Agency|Contractor|Hotel|School|Studio/. Pass when a business node on any fetched page has a non-empty `telephone` string or an `address` with a non-empty `streetAddress` or `addressLocality`, and (when the node has a `name`) that name matches one of the practice's variations with the section 1 matcher ("&" and "and" are the same word on both sides). Warn when a business node exists without those fields, or only with a name that does not match. Fail when there is none. Fix copy: "Add LocalBusiness (or Organization) schema markup to your homepage with your name, phone and address. Most site builders have a setting for this." The service passes the practice's name variations to the check.
- `contact_visible`, label "Your phone, address and hours are easy to find". Evaluated on the full text of the homepage and, when fetched, the contact page (not the 8,000-character slice kept for the report). Phone: a North American number with separators or parentheses, or a leading +1, or a `tel:` href; a match preceded within 12 characters by `#`, `lic`, `license`, `licence`, `ccb`, `permit` or `reg` is a licence number, not a phone. Address: a street number followed by a street name and a street suffix (Street/St, Avenue/Ave, Boulevard/Blvd, Road/Rd, Drive/Dr, Lane/Ln, Way, Court/Ct, Place/Pl, Parkway/Pkwy, Highway/Hwy, Suite/Ste), case-insensitive, with a guard against durations and distances ("a 20 minute drive") and function words between the number and the suffix. Hours: a day name or day range (Mon ... Sun, M-F, M-Sat, weekdays, weekends, daily, every day) followed within 40 characters by a time or a time range joined by `-`, `–`, `—`, `to` or `until`, where a bare clock range without am/pm ("9-5", "8 - 5") counts after a day or an open/hours label unless it is a count (days, weeks, lb, %); or an "open" / "hours" label followed within 40 characters by a time range; or an availability claim (`24/7`, `24 hours a day`, `24-hour emergency/service/support/line`, "open" or "available" near "24 hours", `around the clock`, but never a turnaround such as "we respond within 24 hours"); or JSON-LD `openingHours` / `openingHoursSpecification` on a business node. A day-then-time span that contains a month name or a four-digit year is a dated post or event, not hours. Pass when (phone or address) and hours are found; warn when only one side is; fail when neither. Fix copy names what is missing.
- The service reserves a slot for the contact page: when none of the fact-looking links already taken matches /contact|hours|location|visit/, it fetches `${origin}/contact` as well (tolerating a 404).
- `facts_page` becomes trade-neutral: the pricing pattern gains `fee|fees|estimate|quote`, the crawl pattern that decides which linked pages are fetched (`FACT_PAGE_PATTERN` in `site-text.ts`, shared with the fact drafter) gains the same words plus our-story, who-we-are and what-we-do so those pages are actually reached, and about + services (without pricing) passes; warn when only one of about / pricing / services is present; fail when none. The about / pricing / services words are matched against each page's path and its title with the site name removed (a title segment that appears on more than one page), so a business called "Harbor Plumbing Services" does not pass on its name alone. Fix copy: "Add a plain about page and a services page, and state typical prices, fees or how you quote."
- The check list grows from five to seven. `WebsiteCheckRow.id` and `WebsiteCheck.id` gain the two ids. `build-report.ts` `CHECK_ACTION` gains `business_schema` ("Label your business for machines", effort low, impact medium, track both) and `contact_visible` ("Put your phone, address and hours in plain text", effort low, impact high, track both). `master-website.tsx` budgets its rows: with more than five rows the `why` and `fix` lines use the compact size and the fix is shown inline, so seven failing checks fit one US Letter sheet; the PDF route renders the seven-check case in the manual test. `docs/audit-design/HANDOFF.md` lists seven checks. Older scans stored five checks and keep rendering.

Tests (`tests/core/website-check.test.ts`, extended fixture with a LocalBusiness node, hours and a phone): every phone / address / hours input above, licence numbers rejected, 24/7, per-day tables, JSON-LD-only hours, the name-match rule, the exact seven-id array.

### 8. Words people read

Every string a prospect, client or operator reads uses "business" (never "practice"), "customers" (never "patients"), "team" or "people" (never "providers") and "services" (never "treatments"). Identifiers, URLs, column names and code comments are out of scope (except the two comments noted at the top). Surfaces, all of them: `src/app/layout.tsx` metadata; `src/components/mirror-mark.tsx` tagline; the landing page; `/book` (page, form labels, placeholders, Zod error messages in `audit-request.ts`, the Calendly prefill); `/login`; `/app`; `/change-password`; every dashboard tab and component (`src/components/*`, `src/app/dashboard/**`); every admin page including the "ask" panel placeholder, the client-access panel and the admin nav; every report page under `src/report/pages` and `src/report/scoring.ts` headlines; `src/report/sample-audit.json` (the report-preview page's demo data becomes the Harbor Plumbing demo of section 9); the emails (Pulse, alerts, prospect report, audit request, support); `docs/fact-sheet-intake.md` (the call script); the README and `.env.example` comments.
