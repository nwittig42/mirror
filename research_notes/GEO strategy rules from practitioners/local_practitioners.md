# What Local SEO Practitioners Teach About Getting Local Businesses Recommended by AI (2025-2026)

Evidence grade key used throughout:
- **A** = large measured dataset (10k+ businesses/locations or 1M+ citations), even if vendor-run
- **B** = smaller hands-on test, or structured expert survey (e.g. Whitespark's 47-expert survey)
- **C** = single practitioner opinion / commentary
- **D** = unverified claim seen only in secondary marketing content (do not build product logic on it)

Note on vendor bias: nearly every dataset here comes from a company that sells the fix (Yext = listings, BrightLocal = citations, SOCi = multi-location, Insites/Search Atlas = AI audits). Treat direction of findings as more reliable than exact percentages.

## Q1. Which GBP/listing fields and review signals do practitioners say AI engines use, and what tests show it?

### Takeaway
The measured data agrees on three things: (1) review volume and star rating act as a filter for who gets recommended, (2) the business's own website and third-party listings are the most-cited sources, and Google Business Profile is used as a data source (especially by Google AI Mode/Gemini) even when it isn't the cited link, and (3) AI engines recommend far fewer businesses than the Google 3-pack, so the bar is higher. No public controlled test isolates individual GBP fields (categories, attributes, posts) for AI visibility.

### Cited Findings
- **Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors** (published Nov 6, 2025, Darren Shaw; 47 experts scored 187 factors 0-5; AI Search visibility added as a new scored column for the first time). Top 20 AI visibility factors with scores: 1) Presence on expert-curated "best of" lists (179); 2) Dedicated page for each service (170); 3) Prominence on key industry-relevant domains (167); 4) Quality/authority of unstructured citations: newspaper articles, blog posts, gov sites, industry associations (160); 5) Authority of third-party sites where reviews are present (156); 6) Geographic (city/neighborhood) keyword relevance of content (153); 7) Quantity of unstructured citations (147); 8) High numerical Google ratings, 4-5 (146); 9) Website focus on a specific niche (138); 10) Scannable content structure (137); 11) Diversity of third-party review sites (136); 12) Quantity of unlinked brand mentions (135); 13) Quantity of third-party unstructured reviews, e.g. blogs/newspapers/magazines (133); 14) Volume of quality content (133); 15) Citations from industry-relevant domains (133); 16) Physical address in city of search (132); 17) High numerical third-party ratings (132); 18) Topical keyword relevance across site (132); 19) Quantity of reviews on industry-specific sites (130); 20) Quantity of traditional third-party reviews, e.g. Yelp/TripAdvisor/YP (129). — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (Grade B)
- Notable: no GBP-field factor (categories, attributes, posts) appears in Whitespark's AI top 20; the AI list is dominated by off-site mentions/citations, review-site authority, and website service pages. — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (Grade B)
- Secondary summaries of the same survey report AI Search visibility group weights of On-Page signals 24%, Review signals 16%, Citation signals 13%, with GBP dropping to ~12% for AI (vs. being the top group for the local pack). I could not verify these numbers on the primary page (they are in a chart image). — [Whitespark via search summary](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/); [Reputation.com summary](https://reputation.com/resources/articles/whitespark-2026-three-insights-every-brand-should-know) (Grade B/D)
- Darren Shaw quotes: "In AI SEO, mentions (citations) are the new link." / "3 of the top 5 AI Search visibility factors are citation factors." / On GBP-only data: "I would score it a zero for ChatGPT, but might score it a 3 for Google's AI Mode & Gemini" (because Google has data other LLMs don't). — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (Grade C)
- **SOCi 2026 Local Visibility Index** (~350,000 locations, 2,751 brands): ChatGPT recommended 1.2% of locations, Gemini 11%, Perplexity 7.4%, vs. the same brands appearing in Google's local 3-pack 35.9% of the time. Recommended locations averaged 4.3 stars on ChatGPT, 3.9 on Gemini, 4.1 on Perplexity. Business profile data was ~68% accurate on ChatGPT and Perplexity vs. 100% on Gemini (grounded in Google Maps). In retail, only 45% of top local-search brands carried over into AI recommendations. — [SOCi news](https://soci.ai/news/in-ai-driven-discovery-few-brands-are-chosen-most-disappear/); [Search Engine Land coverage](https://searchengineland.com/ai-local-visibility-report-2026-468085) (Grade A, vendor)
- **Insites "2026 AI Visibility Report"** (May 6, 2026; 10,000 businesses; ChatGPT + Perplexity; 200+ signals, correlation): businesses found by both engines averaged **133 Google reviews vs. 11** for businesses invisible to AI; businesses in the Local Pack were recommended **3.5x more often**; visible businesses had more than double the number of website pages; own website cited in 72% of awareness queries; 93% of businesses "known" by AI but only 55% had accurate details. GBP completeness named as a strong correlate (no figure given). — [NatLawReview press release](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/new-study-10000-local-businesses-reveals-what-ai-actually-uses-decide-who) (Grade A-, vendor, correlational, methodology not disclosed)
- **Search Atlas "near me" study** (104,855 URL citations from OpenAI, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok, Copilot, Google AI Mode; Oct 27-Dec 3, 2025; correlation): semantic relevance of the page to the query was the strongest signal (~0.18-0.19 for OpenAI, Grok, AI Mode; ~0.10 Perplexity; ~0.04 Copilot). "GBP Score" was the strongest non-semantic correlate (~+0.16). Review quality and owner replies: small positive. Map rank and GBP posting frequency: neutral to negative. Domain authority: negative/inconsistent. Freshness mattered most for Gemini (~0.13), then OpenAI (~0.09); negative for Copilot, Grok, AI Mode. Median cited content age ~3-4 years. — [Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/blog/how-llms-rank-local-business/) (Grade A-, vendor, small correlations)
- **Yext** (6.8M citations, 1.6M responses, Gemini/OpenAI/Perplexity, Jul 1-Aug 31 2025, four industries): Websites 44% of citations, Listings 42%, Reviews & social 8%, News/forums/other 6%; Reddit-type forums ~2%. By model: Gemini favors first-party websites; OpenAI leans heavily on third-party listings; Perplexity diversifies across review platforms and local pages. Healthcare: listings = 52.6% of citations (WebMD, Vitals, industry directories dominate). Food service: listings 41.6%, reviews/social 13.3% (highest of any industry). — [Yext blog](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-citations-86-percent-of-sources-are-brand-managed); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251009106549/en/Yext-Research-86-of-AI-Citations-Come-from-Brand-Managed-Sources-Clarifying-How-Marketers-Can-Compete-in-the-AI-Search-Era) (Grade A, vendor)
- iPullRank's read of the Yext data: query parts map to source types — subjective qualifiers ("best") come mainly from review sites; categories ("Indian restaurant") from listings/directories; locations ("Soho") from maps and local pages; objective facts ("patio seating") from websites. Google doesn't cite GBP as a link but uses it as a data source. Facts that appear only on your own site look less trustworthy; corroboration on other sites raises citation odds. — [iPullRank, Dec 16 2025](https://ipullrank.com/local-ai-search-data) (Grade B, interpretation of vendor data)
- **BrightLocal** test (July 2025; 20 searches across 10 niches on AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, ChatGPT): Google AI Mode displayed GBP info even when GBP wasn't the main cited source; Yelp was a source in 33% of searches (Perplexity used Yelp in every industry; Gemini never cited Yelp directly); business websites were the majority source across every engine/industry. — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources) (Grade B, small sample)
- **BrightLocal** Dec 2024 (800 manual ChatGPT local searches): business websites 58% of sources, business mentions 27%, directories 15%; Yelp, Facebook, Google Maps, Reddit and Quora did not appear as sources then. — [BrightLocal research](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/uncovering-chatgpt-search-sources/) (Grade B, now dated; contradicted on Yelp by BrightLocal's own July 2025 test above)
- Joy Hawkins (Sterling Sky, "State of Local SEO in 2026", Jun 26, 2026): Google "AI local packs" surface only ~32% as many businesses as traditional 3-packs (5,943 vs 18,330 unique businesses, Places Scout data), show only 1-2 businesses, no call buttons, appeared on ~7% of tracked keywords, mobile + USA only; 88% of 322 markets had fewer unique businesses in AI packs. Sterling Sky comment: the GBP "What to know" AI section "is typically a mix of ad content and what is on the GBP, usually not the website." — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/) (Grade B)
- Hawkins on ChatGPT traffic: one multi-location client's ChatGPT traffic rose from 0.1% to 2% of its Google traffic; "I'm not seeing it in the data for any of our clients" (that ChatGPT is stealing traffic). — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/) (Grade C)
- Sterling Sky earlier tests found ChatGPT giving invented local details (fake neighborhoods/school) and, in ChatGPT Search, a wrong business ranked first with an outdated directory listing as the source. — [Sterling Sky: Think Twice](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/using-chat-gpt-for-local-content/); [Sterling Sky: ChatGPT Search](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/is-chatgpt-search-the-future-or-just-overhyped/) (Grade B)
- Mike Blumenthal / Darren Shaw (Duda webinar, as summarized): Google's AI looks first at the "local graph" (Maps entity data, business profiles, connected websites) before the wider web. — [Search summary of Near Media/Duda coverage](https://www.nearmedia.co/ep-258-from-keyword-rankings-to-brand-reverence-the-new-local-ai-seo-blueprint-with-darren-shaw/) (Grade C; I did not access the recording)
- GBP Q&A: Google shut the Q&A API on Nov 3, 2025 and began removing public Q&A ~Dec 3, 2025, replacing it with Gemini-powered "Ask Maps" (US/India mobile, March 2026); owners answer aggregated questions that feed Maps answers. Ask Maps reportedly absent for healthcare providers, counseling, dispensaries, locksmiths, movers. Dates conflict between sources. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-quietly-kills-q-a-for-an-ai-button-most-wont-use/); [Slashpost](https://slashpost.ai/blogs/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-qa-removed-2026) (Grade C, secondary)

### Inferences
Rules (IF condition THEN task), with source and grade:
- **R1. IF the business has <~100 Google reviews or <4.2 average THEN run a steady review-request program before any "AI" tactic.** Steps: ask every customer (no screening) via SMS/email within 24h of service, with a direct GBP review link; target a monthly cadence, not a burst. Basis: Insites 133 vs 11 reviews; SOCi 4.1-4.3 avg stars for recommended locations; Whitespark #8 "high Google ratings". (A-/A, correlational.) The "150-review threshold" circulating online is unverified (D) — do not use it as a hard number.
- **R2. IF the engine is Google AI Mode/Gemini THEN GBP completeness is the priority lever; IF ChatGPT/Perplexity THEN website + third-party listings are.** Steps: for Google, fill primary + secondary categories, services, hours, attributes, description, photos; for ChatGPT, make sure listings and website state the same facts. Basis: Shaw "zero for ChatGPT, 3 for AI Mode & Gemini"; SOCi Gemini 100% vs ChatGPT 68% data accuracy; Yext OpenAI leans on listings, Gemini on websites. (B/A.)
- **R3. IF the business doesn't appear in the Google local pack for its main "service + city" query THEN fix local-pack ranking first.** Basis: Insites 3.5x recommendation rate for local-pack businesses (A-, correlational). Sterling Sky warns AI packs show only 1-2 businesses, so the bar is rising (B).
- **R4. IF AI engines state wrong facts (hours, address, services) THEN correct the source listings, not the AI.** Steps: run "best [service] in [city]" and "[business name] hours/services" in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Mode, Claude; record wrong facts and which source each cites; fix that source plus GBP, website, and aggregators. Basis: Sterling Sky audit checklist recommends checking ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, AI Overviews for accuracy ([Sterling Sky audit checklist](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/local-seo-audit-checklist/), C); SOCi 68% accuracy on ChatGPT/Perplexity (A); Insites only 55% accurate (A-).
- **R5. IF the category is healthcare (dentist, med spa) THEN prioritize health directories; IF restaurant THEN prioritize reviews/social and food/lifestyle sites.** Basis: Yext healthcare listings 52.6% (WebMD, Vitals), food service reviews/social 13.3% (A, vendor).
- GBP posts and posting frequency appear as low-value: Search Atlas finds posting frequency neutral-to-negative, and Whitespark's myth list includes quantity/frequency of Google Posts and keywords in Posts. Treat posts as conversion hygiene, not an AI lever.

### Gaps
- No public controlled experiment isolating individual GBP fields (secondary categories, attributes, services list, photos count) for AI recommendations. All evidence is correlational or expert opinion.
- Whitespark's AI group-weighting percentages are in a chart image; the 24/16/13/12% figures come from secondary summaries only.
- No primary data found for Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, or Copilot specifically in local recommendation studies (only Search Atlas includes Copilot, and only at URL level). The common claim that ChatGPT relies on Bing/Foursquare data was seen only as "reports suggest 60-70% of ChatGPT local results come from Foursquare" inside BrightLocal's article, with no primary source. — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources) (D)
- Could not access video/podcast timestamps; Near Media EP 231 and EP 258 with Darren Shaw are likely primary sources but transcripts weren't retrieved: [EP 231](https://nearmedia.co/ep-231-local-search-in-the-age-of-ai-darren-shaw-on-the-2026-ranking-factors), [EP 258](https://www.nearmedia.co/ep-258-from-keyword-rankings-to-brand-reverence-the-new-local-ai-seo-blueprint-with-darren-shaw/).
- Search Engine Land (SOCi coverage, Darren Shaw's "new playbook for localized AI search optimization") returned 403 and couldn't be read directly.

## Q2. How do practitioners find which directories and "best of" lists AI cites for a city + category, and how do they get included?

### Takeaway
Being on expert-curated "best of" lists is the single highest-scored AI visibility factor in Whitespark's 2026 survey. The method practitioners teach is simple: run the actual "best [service] in [city]" prompts in each engine and in Google, record every cited list and directory, then pursue inclusion. Directory mix is strongly industry-specific (dental and legal directories dominate for those niches).

### Cited Findings
- "Presence of Business on Expert Curated Best of and Similar Lists" is the #1 AI Search visibility factor (score 179); "Prominence on Key Industry-Relevant Domains" is #3 (167). — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B)
- Darren Shaw's method (as summarized): search "best [keyword] in [city]", see which pages appear, then work out how to get your business included. He describes best-of listings as a surefire way to rank better in local results. — [Uberall Local Beat / search summary](https://uberall.com/en-us/resources/local-beat/master-local-seo-whitespark) (C; summary, not verified verbatim)
- BrightLocal July 2025: all LLMs used directories in every industry. Dentistry: ChatGPT drew exclusively on ten dental directories; Toprateddentist.com appeared in Gemini, AI Mode and ChatGPT. Legal: Superlawyers.com and Findlaw.com heavily used by ChatGPT and Perplexity. MapQuest frequently used by AI Mode and Perplexity. Yelp in 33% of searches. Instagram cited by AI Mode and Perplexity; Facebook by AI Mode and ChatGPT; YouTube influenced Gemini and Perplexity. Hospitality: blogs/lifestyle sites (e.g. Time Out). — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources) (B)
- BrightLocal Dec 2024: Three Best Rated was the top directory (~24% of ChatGPT directory sources); Wikipedia was 39% of "business mentions" sources. — [BrightLocal research](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/uncovering-chatgpt-search-sources/) (B, dated)
- BrightLocal's recommended steps: build foundational citations including niche/industry directories; prioritize complete GBP; use data aggregators (Data Axle, Foursquare, Neustar); keep NAP consistent; pursue local mentions/PR from news sites, community blogs, lifestyle publications. — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources) (C, vendor)
- Yext: healthcare citations dominated by WebMD, Vitals and industry-specific directories. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-citations-86-percent-of-sources-are-brand-managed) (A)
- Brandon Schmidt (Whitespark contributor): "brand mentions & community connections are even more important." Colan Nielsen: his team is doing "reverse engineering across the major platforms" to see what LLMs look for, and stresses citation indexation. — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (C)

### Inferences
- **R6. IF a business is not on the lists AI cites for "best [service] in [city]" THEN build a target list and pursue each.** Exact steps: (1) Run "best [service] in [city]", "top-rated [service] near [neighborhood]", "[service] [city] recommendations" in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Mode, Copilot, Claude, and Google web search; (2) export every cited URL; (3) classify each as: editorial list (local news/magazine/blog), aggregator list (Three Best Rated, Expertise-style), industry directory, general directory; (4) for directories: claim/create a listing with identical NAP, categories, services; (5) for editorial lists: email the author with a reason to include (awards, specialty, notable reviews, a local story); (6) re-run monthly. Basis: Shaw method (C) + Whitespark #1 factor (B) + BrightLocal directory findings (B).
- **R7. IF dentist THEN claim/complete dental directories (e.g. Toprateddentist.com, and health directories like WebMD/Vitals); IF law firm THEN Super Lawyers, FindLaw (and equivalents); IF restaurant THEN Yelp, Time Out-style lifestyle sites, Instagram; IF any category THEN Yelp, Facebook, MapQuest and data aggregators (Data Axle, Foursquare).** Basis: BrightLocal July 2025 (B), Yext industry split (A).
- Paid "best of" placements: some aggregator lists charge for inclusion. If you pay, the listing may need disclosure as advertising; flag as an FTC endorsement-disclosure risk (inference, no source found specifically on paid AI-cited lists).

### Gaps
- No source gave a step-by-step outreach script or success rates for getting onto editorial best-of lists.
- No 2025-2026 data on Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, or Yelp's direct impact on ChatGPT/Copilot answers from primary research.
- No med spa or home-services directory citation data found (only dental, legal, hospitality, fitness, vet in BrightLocal).

## Q3. What review-request and review-response rules do they give (volume, recency, keyword mentions, FTC-compliant)?

### Takeaway
Volume and rating correlate with AI recommendation, and review presence on multiple authoritative third-party sites (not just Google) is a top-ranked AI factor. Asking everyone without screening is mandatory under both the FTC rule and Google policy. Owner replies show a small positive correlation. I found no measured evidence that keyword-stuffed review text improves AI visibility.

### Cited Findings
- Whitespark AI factors on reviews: #5 authority of third-party sites with reviews (156), #8 high Google ratings (146), #11 diversity of third-party review sites (136), #13 third-party unstructured reviews in blogs/newspapers (133), #17 high third-party ratings (132), #19 reviews on industry-specific sites (130), #20 traditional reviews on Yelp/TripAdvisor/YP (129). — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B)
- Whitespark: the biggest 2026 changes in the local pack are increased importance of review signals and behavioral signals. — [Whitespark via search summary](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B)
- Insites: 133 vs 11 average Google reviews for AI-visible vs invisible businesses. — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/new-study-10000-local-businesses-reveals-what-ai-actually-uses-decide-who) (A-)
- SOCi: recommended locations average 4.3 stars (ChatGPT), 4.1 (Perplexity), 3.9 (Gemini). — [SOCi](https://soci.ai/news/in-ai-driven-discovery-few-brands-are-chosen-most-disappear/) (A)
- Search Atlas: review quality and owner replies show a small positive correlation with LLM citation rank. — [Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/blog/how-llms-rank-local-business/) (A-, small effect)
- Yext/iPullRank: subjective "best" queries pull mainly from review sites, but review/social citations are declining relative to websites and listings; Christian Ward (Yext) calls it "an existential threat" for review sites. — [iPullRank](https://ipullrank.com/local-ai-search-data) (B)
- FTC Consumer Review Rule (effective Oct 21, 2024) bars conditioning any compensation or incentive on a reviewer expressing a particular sentiment, and targets selective display/gating that hides negative reviews. Civil penalties reported up to $53,088 per violation; FTC is sending warning letters (2026). — [Momentic primer](https://momenticmarketing.com/blog/seo-primer-ftc-consumer-reviews); [Benesch law alert](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/pdf/) (C, secondary legal summaries)
- Mike Blumenthal / Near Media: the FTC's $4.2M Fashion Nova settlement "kills review gating"; Near Media also published FTC review guidelines for business marketers. — [Near Media: $4.2M fine](https://nearmedia.co/ftc-drops-a-4-2-review-abuse-fine/); [Near Media: FTC guidelines](https://nearmedia.co/ftcs-business-marketer-guidelines) (C)
- Google policy: merchants must not discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones. — [Search summary of Google policy via idukki glossary](https://idukki.io/glossary/review-gating) (C, secondary; verify against Google's own policy page)
- Sterling Sky warns of a local SEO mistake that cost a business all its reviews (review policy risk). — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/mistake-that-could-cost-all-your-reviews/) (C; details not read)

### Inferences
- **R8. IF a review-request flow screens customers ("Were you happy? Yes → Google, No → private form") THEN remove the screen.** Send the same public review link to every customer. Risk: FTC rule + Google policy violation, possible review removal. (C, legal.)
- **R9. IF offering any incentive for reviews THEN stop, or make it unconditional and disclosed** — never tied to positive sentiment; note Google's policy is stricter (prohibits incentivized reviews outright, per practitioner consensus; not verified on a primary Google page in this research).
- **R10. IF all reviews are on Google only THEN diversify** to 2-3 authoritative third-party sites relevant to the category (Yelp; Healthgrades/Vitals/WebMD-type for health; Avvo/Super Lawyers-type for legal; TripAdvisor for restaurants). Basis: Whitespark #5, #11, #19 (B).
- **R11. IF reviews go unanswered THEN reply to all reviews**, especially negative, factually and without disclosing private info (HIPAA risk for dentists/med spas: never confirm someone is a patient). Basis: Search Atlas small positive correlation for owner replies (A-, weak). HIPAA point is an inference, not sourced here.
- **R12. Review text keywords:** asking customers to mention the service and city is commonly taught, but I found no 2025-2026 measured evidence it changes AI recommendations; scripting exact wording risks looking fake. Safe version: prompt with open questions ("what service did you get and how did it go?"), not scripts.

### Gaps
- No primary study measured review recency or review-text keyword effects on AI recommendations.
- No practitioner-published "X reviews per month" target for AI specifically.
- Google's own review policy page and the FTC rule text were not fetched directly; quotes rely on secondary legal/blog summaries.

## Q4. What should a local service page contain to be cited (prices, provider credentials, before/after, FAQs)?

### Takeaway
"Dedicated page for each service" is the #2 AI visibility factor in Whitespark's survey, and the strongest measured signal in the Search Atlas study is how closely the page's content matches the query. Practitioners say: one page per service, with city/neighborhood relevance, scannable structure, specific facts (the kind AI uses for "objective" questions), and facts that match listings and third-party sites. Specific evidence on prices, credentials, or before/after photos was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Whitespark AI factors: #2 dedicated page per service (170); #6 city/neighborhood keyword relevance (153); #9 website focus on a niche (138); #10 scannable content structure, headings/bullets/images (137); #14 volume of quality content (133); #18 topical keyword relevance (132). — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B)
- Darren Shaw: "Just like humans, the robots also don't want to read your drivel." — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (C)
- Search Atlas: semantic relevance is the strongest predictor; OpenAI and Gemini favor page-level content over homepages; Perplexity relies more on homepages. — [Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/blog/how-llms-rank-local-business/) (A-)
- iPullRank/Yext: objective facts ("patio seating") are pulled from websites; create dedicated pages for each neighborhood, city, region served; local pages on subdomains form a large share of citations because they're concise; facts corroborated off-site are more trusted; don't block AI crawlers. — [iPullRank](https://ipullrank.com/local-ai-search-data) (B)
- Insites: AI-visible businesses had more than double the website pages; stale sites "significantly less likely" to be recommended. — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/new-study-10000-local-businesses-reveals-what-ai-actually-uses-decide-who) (A-)
- BrightLocal: optimize website with NAP, services, hours, FAQs and reviews. — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources) (C)
- Joy Hawkins: "Change your content strategy to focus on writing content that your competitors haven't written." Also: "Google's AI Overviews has crushed blog/article traffic." — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/) (C)
- Brian Barwig (Whitespark contributor): "Make sure you do not block AI from crawling your websites." Elizabeth Rule: "I do treat AIO as featured snippets." — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (C)
- Sterling Sky warns against using ChatGPT to write local content because it invents local facts. — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/using-chat-gpt-for-local-content/) (B)

### Inferences
- **R13. IF services are listed on one combined page THEN split into one page per service** (e.g. /botox, /lip-filler, /dental-implants, /water-heater-repair). Each page: H1 = service + city; short direct answer paragraph up top; who performs it (name + credentials); what it costs (price or range, "from $X"); duration, recovery/turnaround; FAQs written as real customer questions; neighborhoods served; NAP + hours; link to reviews mentioning that service. Prices/credentials/before-after are inferred as "objective facts" AI pulls from websites (per iPullRank's query mapping), not separately tested. (B/C.)
- **R14. IF serving multiple cities/neighborhoods THEN create a real page per location with unique local facts** (not city-name swaps; Whitespark lists geographic relevance #6, iPullRank recommends dedicated pages). Scaled thin "service × city" doorway pages remain a Google spam-policy risk (inference).
- **R15. IF robots.txt blocks GPTBot/OAI-SearchBot/PerplexityBot/ClaudeBot/Google-Extended THEN unblock** (Barwig, iPullRank). (C.)
- **R16. IF site content hasn't been updated in a year+ THEN refresh service pages (prices, staff, dates).** Basis: Insites stale-site finding (A-), Gemini freshness correlation 0.13 (A-). Note freshness is negative/neutral for Copilot, Grok, AI Mode in Search Atlas, so don't fake dates.
- Med spa / medical before-after photos and claims: flag FTC health-claim substantiation and state medical-advertising rules; testimonials need to be typical or disclosed (inference; not sourced in this research).

### Gaps
- No test isolating the effect of listing prices, provider credentials, or before/after galleries on AI citation.
- No primary data on service × city page formats beyond expert opinion.

## Q5. What do practitioners say is a myth for local AI visibility?

### Takeaway
The most consistent "doesn't matter (much)" list across practitioners and data: schema markup, llms.txt, technical SEO/Core Web Vitals, domain authority, GBP post frequency, and keywords in GBP Posts/Q&A. Also contested: that ChatGPT is already taking meaningful local traffic (Hawkins sees little), and that Reddit/forums dominate local AI answers (Yext: ~2%).

### Cited Findings
- Darren Shaw on schema for AI: "currently remain unconvinced that it's helpful." Schema markup also appears on Whitespark's local SEO myths list (#27, score 49). — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B/C)
- Whitespark myths list includes keywords in Google Posts (#28), keywords in Google Q&A (#34), and quantity/frequency of Google Posts. — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (B)
- Insites: weakest or no correlation with AI visibility for technical SEO, Core Web Vitals, page speed, structured data, LLMs.txt, and FAQ schema. — [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/new-study-10000-local-businesses-reveals-what-ai-actually-uses-decide-who) (A-)
- Search Atlas: domain authority negative/inconsistent; schema weak (small positive only for Copilot and AI Mode); GBP posting frequency neutral to negative. — [Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/blog/how-llms-rank-local-business/) (A-)
- Yext: forums like Reddit ~2% of local citations. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/blog/ai-citations-86-percent-of-sources-are-brand-managed) (A); but Hawkins recommends Reddit/YouTube as a long-term expertise play and BrightLocal saw YouTube influencing Gemini/Perplexity — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/); [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/blog/ai-search-using-listings-sources)
- Hawkins: ChatGPT "stealing the traffic" — "I'm not seeing it in the data for any of our clients"; Google AI Overviews, by contrast, have hurt blog/article traffic. — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/) (C)
- Greg Gifford: "this is still so early" — not doing anything specifically new for AI. — [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) (C)
- Darren Shaw (per AdviceLocal summary): AI visibility has a surprisingly smaller impact on local search than expected, as Google search still dominates. Reputation.com claims local and AI signals have effectively merged. — [AdviceLocal](https://www.advicelocal.com/?p=20716); [Reputation.com](https://reputation.com/resources/articles/whitespark-2026-three-insights-every-brand-should-know) (C, conflicting)

### Inferences
- **R17. IF a vendor pitches llms.txt, FAQ schema, or Core Web Vitals work as the AI-visibility fix for a local business THEN deprioritize it** behind reviews, best-of lists, service pages, and citations. (A-/B.) Schema is cheap hygiene, not a lever.
- **R18. IF a client wants daily GBP posts "for AI" THEN redirect effort** to listings accuracy and off-site mentions. (B/A-.)
- **R19. IF measuring AI success THEN track AI referral traffic in GA4 and monthly prompt checks per engine, not just rank.** Hawkins/Sterling Sky use GA4 for AI traffic and conversions; she recommends Seer's Looker Studio template. — [Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/the-state-of-local-seo-in-2026/) (C). Note Findloc (promotional, unverified) reports 391 of 690 markets had a different #1 AI answer a week later, so single checks are noisy — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/findloc-ai-business-location-research) (D).
