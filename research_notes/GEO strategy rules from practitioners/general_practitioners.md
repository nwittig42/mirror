# GEO/AEO Practitioner Rules (2025-2026): What Leading Practitioners Teach, as Testable IF/THEN Rules

Scope note: ~17 searches and fetches. Lenny's Podcast transcript, Aleyda Solis's checklist page (403) and most Growth Memo posts are paywalled or blocked, so several items rely on show notes or second-hand summaries. These are flagged. Evidence grades used below:
- **[T]** tested or measured by the source (experiment or their own dataset)
- **[C]** correlational data
- **[O]** opinion or practitioner experience
- **[SP]** self-promotional: the source sells a tracking or optimization tool tied to the claim
- Applies to: **L** = local businesses, **O** = online/SaaS/ecommerce, **B** = both

## Q1. What exact rules and checklists do practitioners give?

### Takeaway
The rules that hold up across sources are: (1) be in the search index and rank, since about 88% of ChatGPT citations come from its search channel; (2) match page titles and URL slugs to the prompt and its fan-out sub-queries; (3) put the answer and key facts near the top, in self-contained chunks; (4) get mentioned on many third-party sources (Reddit, YouTube, listicles, reviews) rather than chasing one #1 spot; (5) keep commercial pages fresh. Local businesses are a separate case: Google review count and Business Profile completeness dominate.

### Cited Findings

**Indexing and ranking (gate rules)**
- RULE: IF a page is not in Google/Bing's index or doesn't rank for the topic, THEN fix indexing and ranking first. About 88% of ChatGPT citations come from its "search" retrieval channel (88.46% citation rate for that channel, vs 1.93% for Reddit and 0.51% for YouTube). Ahrefs: "content must be in that selection pool, which means it needs to rank." Ahrefs (Louise Linehan, Ryan Law), 1.4M prompts, Apr 2026. [C] [SP: Brand Radar] B. — [Ahrefs: Why ChatGPT Cites One Page Over Another](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/)
- "Not indexed by Google? You will struggle to show up on ChatGPT." "OpenAI is indexing the web at retrieval-time." ChatGPT's fan-out queries overlap 39% with the Google SERP (sample of 1,000 SERPs). Josh Blyskal (Profound), BrightonSEO / Tech SEO Connect deck. [C] [SP] B. — [Blyskal deck: 250M AI search responses](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)
- Counterpoint: classic SEO metrics "explain only 4-7% of citations" (1,311 pages). Doubling SEO metrics yields "only 25-40% more citations." Blyskal says ~93% of citation variance comes from "other factors like content quality." [C] [SP] — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)
- About 62% of LLM citations come from outside the top 10 search results. Even top-ranking pages appear in AI results only 19-25% of the time (third-party datasets cited in Mike King's deck). [C, second-hand] — [iPullRank Tech SEO Connect deck](https://ipullrank.com/tech-seo-connect) (summary via search snippet; not opened)
- ChatGPT ran JavaScript during retrieval, per Blyskal's slide. Other engines' values couldn't be extracted. — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)

**Titles, URLs, meta: "the pitch"**
- RULE: IF the title of the page meant to win a prompt doesn't closely match the prompt wording or its fan-out sub-questions, THEN rewrite the title to mirror them. Similarity between the prompt and the title was 0.602 for cited pages vs 0.484 for non-cited pages, and 0.656 between fan-out queries and cited titles. "The pages that get cited are the ones whose titles and content match the questions ChatGPT is asking." [C] B. — [Ahrefs 1.4M prompts](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/)
- RULE: IF the URL slug is an ID or non-descriptive, THEN use a natural-language slug of 4-7 words. Ahrefs: 89.78% citation rate with natural-language slugs vs 81.11% without. Blyskal: natural-language URLs get "11.4% more citations" (50k top vs 50k bottom pages), and URLs semantically close to the query get "up to 5% more." [C] B. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/); [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)
- RULE: IF the meta description is a teaser, THEN "spoil your content in your meta-description": state the answer. Rationale per Blyskal: "ChatGPT cannot read the full article before citing, only a snippet"; engines "only read an ~100 character snip from each page"; "Title, Description, URL and Snippet are your only pitch to get cited." [C/O] [SP] B. — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)

**Answer placement and page structure**
- RULE: IF the direct answer or key facts sit below the first ~30% of the page, THEN move them up. Kevin Indig found that 44.2% of 18,012 verified ChatGPT citations came from the first 30% of a page, and content buried deep was ~2.5x less likely to be cited. [C] B. (Paywalled; figures from search snippet.) — [Growth Memo: The science of how AI picks its sources](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-science-of-how-ai-picks-its-sources?open=false)
- RULE: IF a page is a sprawling "ultimate guide," THEN consider splitting it into focused pages. Indig's April 2026 analysis of 815,000 query-page pairs suggests "ultimate guides" earn fewer citations than focused, shorter pages. [C] B. (Snippet only.) — [Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-science-of-how-ai-picks-its-sources?open=false)
- RULE: IF content is walls of text, THEN break it into self-contained semantic chunks with clear headings, write in subject-predicate-object sentences, and embed specific verifiable numbers. King: "LLMs retrieve and reason over small chunks of text, not whole documents." More relevant passages work like more raffle tickets. LLMs favor "salient, distinctive, non-generic content." Mike King (iPullRank), AI Mode webinar. [O] B. — [iPullRank AI Mode webinar recap](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap)
- "The content that wins in AEO is content that answers directly and takes a position." "LLMs are eager to consume structured, opinionated content that delivers them value upfront." Blyskal. [O/C] — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)
- RULE: IF a key fact about the brand appears only on the brand's own site, THEN get it repeated on independent authoritative domains, since "retrieval favors corroborated claims." King. Use consistent terms and internal links across topic clusters. [O] B. — [iPullRank recap](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap)
- RULE: IF there are no image, video or audio assets for a topic, THEN create them, since "there is no moat in text alone." King. [O] — [iPullRank recap](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap)
- Aleyda Solis's AI Search Optimization Checklist (updated May 2026, with worksheet) has a table mapping each gap to a fix. Examples from the snippet: IF the brand is missing from commercial prompts THEN add comparison content; IF content can't be retrieved at all THEN fix crawl and index access. The June 2025 version had 8 areas: chunk-level retrieval, answer synthesis, citation-worthiness, topical breadth, multimodal, authority signals, personalization resilience, crawlability. The July 2025 version had 10 steps. [O] B. **Fan-out**
- RULE: IF the target prompt is broad, THEN simulate its fan-out sub-queries (Qforia, Brand Radar) and make sure page sections or titles answer them. Ahrefs and King both recommend this. Caveat from a third-party guide (NoGood): don't build a separate page per fan-out query, because fan-out queries are "inherently unstable and personalized." Blyskal: "89% of prompts generate 2-3 search queries." [C/O] B. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/); [iPullRank](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap); [NoGood](https://nogood.io/?p=47679); [Blyskal](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)

**Freshness**
- RULE: IF a commercial page cited (or meant to be cited) by ChatGPT hasn't been updated in more than 6 months, THEN refresh it. AirOps: over 60% of commercial pages cited by ChatGPT were updated in the past 6 months; informational queries surfaced older pages more often. [C] [SP] O. — [AirOps stale content report](https://airops.com/report/the-impact-of-stale-content-on-ai-visibility)
- "50% of top cited content is less than 13 weeks old"; "Answer Engines have a recency bias"; engines "overwhelmingly prefer recent YouTube videos." Blyskal. [C] [SP] — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)

Ahrefs' 17M-citation study puts the average age of cited content at 2.9 years. Ahrefs concludes relevance matters more than age, though freshness does help for news (~200 vs ~300 days median). Seer (47,097 citations, Mar-Jun 2026): 73% of ChatGPT citations were updated within the last year. A Profound-reported figure says Claude used live web search on only 36.6% of prompts, so freshness matters less there (second-hand). ### Inferences
- Rules that are cheap to automate from audit data: title-to-prompt similarity check, URL slug check, "answer in first 30%" check, meta description contains the answer, last-modified date older than 6 months on commercial pages, review count vs AI-recommended local competitors, GBP completeness, and missing integration/use-case/help pages.
- The strongest-evidence levers are correlational at scale (Ahrefs, Profound, Insites). Almost nothing is a controlled experiment. Graphite's control-group method is the only explicitly causal approach, and its results aren't public in the sources I could open.
- Engine matters. YouTube helps Google surfaces, not ChatGPT. Freshness matters for ChatGPT and news, less for Claude. Reddit is heavily used by Perplexity. A to-do list should tag each task with the engines it helps.

## Q2. How do they pick prompts, prioritize and measure?

### Takeaway
Ethan Smith's process is the most fully described. Build prompts by clustering competitor keywords into questions, add Reddit, sales calls and Bing grounding queries, run each prompt 5-10 times, analyze source categories rather than domains, then measure with a "how did you hear about us" survey. Rand Fishkin's data says to track how often you appear, not your rank.

### Cited Findings
- **Prompt selection (Ethan Smith via Reforge, Oct 2026):** (1) Take a competitor URL that ranks for your topic, pull its keywords, and have an LLM group them into sub-themes and rewrite each group as a question. "Three hundred variations become fifteen prompts." (2) Find Reddit threads on the topic, sorted by upvotes, as a volume proxy. Use Gemini, not Claude, because "Google has a larger Reddit corpus." (3) Use LLM brainstorming only as a supplement. (4) Check Bing Webmaster Tools grounding queries (Copilot). (5) Transcribe sales calls and extract pre-purchase questions. [O] O. — [Reforge AEO Playbook](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook)
- An earlier summary says Smith starts from competitors' **paid search** data. — [Graphite/secondary summaries](https://graphite.io/five-percent/the-ultimate-guide-to-aeo-on-lennys-podcast)
- **Tracking rules:** "Ask each prompt five to ten times to understand the distribution of answers." Separate branded from non-branded prompts. "Build your strategy around categories, not individual sites." [O] — [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook)
- **Variance evidence:** Across ~3,000 runs from 600 volunteers, ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI gave the same brand list twice in under 1 in 100 cases. Order and list length varied too. How often a brand appears is more stable than its rank. Fishkin warns against tools that claim precise rank. Co-author Patrick O'Donnell is CTO of Gumshoe.ai; not peer-reviewed. [T] — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-recommendation-lists-rarely-repeat-study-468076); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242)
- **Citation concentration:** 30 domains capture ~67% of ChatGPT citations within each vertical (Indig, ~98k citation rows / 1.2M responses; second-hand Portuguese summary). 91% of AI citations appeared on only one of ChatGPT, Perplexity or AIO (3.7M URLs). [C] — [Conversion.com.br](https://www.conversion.com.br/blog/chatgpt-citacoes-dominios-concentracao-2); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/kevin-indig-documents-ai-search-measurement-gap-00cb8aaf)
- **Gap finding:** Use Brand Radar (or similar) to find prompts where competitors are cited and you aren't, then review those prompts' fan-out queries (Ahrefs). Ask an AI directly what it knows about your brand, how it compares you to competitors, and what's missing (Smith). King: collect the AI Mode data first (DemandSphere, FetchSERP), vectorize it, then optimize passages. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/why-chatgpt-cites-pages/); [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook); [iPullRank](https://ipullrank.com/ai-mode-webinar-recap)
- **Measurement:** Use an on-arrival survey asking how the visitor heard about you. If they pick AI, ask which assistant, then what prompt. At Graphite, self-reported AI attribution was "ten times higher than last-touch attribution." Pair with mixed media modeling. Use control groups for experiments. [T-internal] [SP] — [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook); [Lenny's episode page, 43:06](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-aeo-ethan-smith)
- **Budget split:** For companies with reasonable SEO authority (about Series A or later), start at roughly two-thirds SEO and one-third AEO. [O] — [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook)
- **Why prioritize AEO:** LLM traffic converts 6x better than Google (Webflow's experience, per Smith). Early-stage startups can appear in answers "almost instantly" after a Reddit mention. [O/anecdotal] [SP] — [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/the-ultimate-guide-to-aeo-on-lennys-podcast)

### Inferences
- Priority order for a to-do list, as implied by these sources: (1) fix indexing and crawlability; (2) for each non-branded prompt where you're missing, look at the source categories that are cited; (3) fix owned pages (title, slug, answer first, freshness); (4) earn mentions in the categories that dominate; (5) remeasure with 5-10 runs per prompt and report appearance rate, not rank.
- Because the top ~30 domains capture most citations in a vertical, "find the domains cited for my lost prompts and get on them" is a well-supported tactic, even though no one gave an outreach script.

### Gaps
- No practitioner gave a numeric scoring formula for task priority.
- No source specified how many prompts to track per business. Smith's "300 keywords to 15 prompts" is the only ratio found.

## Q3. What outreach or page templates do they share?

### Takeaway
No source I could retrieve published a cold outreach email template for getting into third-party listicles. The shared "templates" are behavioral: Reddit engagement rules, AMAs, and page anatomy (title, slug, meta, answer first, chunks, FAQs).

### Cited Findings
- **Reddit template (Smith):** one real account; say who you are and where you work; give genuinely helpful answers; no automation or fake accounts. Respond to criticism thoughtfully, not defensively. Run AMAs with moderators to correct specific misunderstandings; a dog-food brand's vet AMA produced a "clear shift" in sentiment (no number given). "The approach that actually works on Reddit is engagement, not manipulation." [O] — [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/the-ultimate-guide-to-aeo-on-lennys-podcast); [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook)
- **Reforge on outreach:** the page "doesn't describe cold outreach to publishers or editors." Its guidance is limited to engaging with existing earned content and to owned profiles. — [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/aeo-playbook)
- **Page anatomy, assembled from Blyskal, Ahrefs, Indig and King:** a title mirroring the prompt or fan-out wording; a 4-7 word natural-language slug; a meta description that states the answer; the answer and key data in the first ~30% of the page; self-contained H2 chunks; a clear position; specific numbers; FAQs (especially on product pages); video where Google surfaces matter; regular updates. — sources as in Q1.
- **Recommendation loop (Blyskal):** "Track queries, create AI-conducive content, and monitor and mimic citations"; build on Reddit with your brand community; "think of products as JSON." [O] [SP] — [Blyskal deck](https://speakerdeck.com/techseoconnect/josh-blyskal-we-analyzed-250000-000-ai-search-responses-heres-what-we-found)

### Inferences
- "Mimic citations" implies a rule: for each lost prompt, inspect the top cited pages' format (listicle, blog, docs, forum) and length, then match the format on your own site and pursue inclusion on those exact pages.

### Gaps
- No verbatim listicle-author or editor outreach email found from any named practitioner (Gotch, Sturm, Diggity and Patel weren't retrieved).

## Q4. Where do they disagree, and what do they call myths?

### Takeaway
The main fights are: self-promotional listicles (they work short-term but carry spam risk); freshness (Profound says strong recency bias, Ahrefs says relevance matters more than age); how much classic SEO matters (Ahrefs says it's the gate, Profound says it explains only 4-7%); Reddit's real citation share; and whether rank tracking in AI is meaningful at all (Fishkin says no).

### Cited Findings
- **Self-promotional "best X" listicles:** Lily Ray's analysis of 100 B2B "best [category] software" queries found self-promotional listicles were cited in 80 AI Overview prompts, but ~69% of the time the cited brand was left out of the recommendation. Recommended brands had far more referring domains and AI mentions. She reports 30-50% visibility drops on sites with mass-produced "best X" and "alternatives" pages and spammy schema (no named Google update). In a May 2026 conversation with Ross Hudgens (Siege Media), she said self-promotional listicles and prompt injection via "summarize with AI" buttons are already treated as spam by Google and Microsoft.
