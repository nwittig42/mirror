# Small-Business Marketing Budgets, Comparable Service Prices, Customer Value and Pricing Norms (US, 2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[SURVEY]** = primary survey or dataset with a stated sample.
- **[VENDOR]** = vendor, agency or aggregator claim with no disclosed method.
- **[OPINION]** = practitioner opinion.
- **(unverified)** = figure came from a search-result summary; I did not open the primary page to confirm it.

Overall caution for the report writer: hard, by-trade survey data is thin. Most by-trade numbers in circulation are agency-blog figures with no method. The strongest primary sources I opened are LocaliQ (budgets), BrightLocal and Yext (consumer AI use), Bluehost (owner AI awareness) and SE Ranking (SEO retainer prices).

## 1. What do US small businesses spend on marketing, by size band and by trade?

### Takeaway
A large share of small businesses run on a total marketing budget under $1,000 a month, and the smallest (10 or fewer staff) skew under $500 a month, so a $495 to $1,995 monthly service would equal or exceed the whole marketing budget of many of them. Percent-of-revenue "rules" (roughly 2% to 12% depending on trade) are mostly advice, not measured spend.

### Cited Findings
Size bands and overall budgets
- [SURVEY] LocaliQ small business marketing trends report (730+ owners and marketers, 74% US or Canada; page last updated Dec 3, 2025; field dates not stated): 5% have no marketing budget; 33% have a budget under $1,000 per month; businesses with 10 or fewer employees were 31% more likely to have a budget under $500 per month and 31% more likely to have no full-time marketing staff. — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)
- [SURVEY] Same report: 49% planned to increase budgets in 2025, 35% hold, 16% decrease (56% of decreasers cite the economy). 61% are not investing in SEO; only 19% use reputation management; 28% use online listings. 60% work with at least one marketing partner, and 73% of those use more than one. 65% say referrals are their best lead source (75% for firms with 10 or fewer staff). — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)
- Conflict to note: a search summary of the same LocaliQ report gave "38% had monthly budgets under $2,500" rather than "33% under $1,000". The fetched page gave the 33% under $1,000 figure; the two may be different bands or different report years. (unverified which) — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)
- [VENDOR, older] One article cites a 2023 survey putting average small business marketing spend at $534 per month (about $6,400 a year). Older than the 2024 cutoff and secondhand (unverified). — [RevenueMemo](https://www.revenuememo.com/p/small-business-marketing-budget-statistics)
- [OPINION / secondhand] "SBA recommends 7-8% of gross revenue for businesses under $5M revenue" is repeated widely, including by Clio, but I could not find it on an official SBA page. Treat as folklore attributed to the SBA. — [Clio](https://www.clio.com/blog/marketing-budget-for-small-law-firm/)
- [SURVEY, contrast only] Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey: marketing at 7.7% of company revenue, sample is mostly firms above $1B revenue, so not a small-business benchmark (unverified at primary source). — [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2025-marketing-planning)

By trade
- Home services [VENDOR, no method]: marketing as share of revenue by trade: HVAC 8-12%, plumbing 7-10%, roofing 10-15%, electrical 8-11%, general contractor 8-12%, house cleaning 12-18%, landscaping 10-14%, painting 10-14%. Article dated Apr 29, 2026; no source given for this table. — [Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/home-services-marketing-benchmarks-by-trade/)
- Home services [OPINION]: ACCA (HVAC trade association) blog suggests roughly 8-10% of revenue for about 10% growth and 12-14% for 15-20%+ growth (unverified at page level). — [ACCA blog](https://hvac-blog.acca.org/its-not-a-sprint-its-a-marathon-setting-realistic-expectations-of-marketing-in-the-home-services-industry/)
- Legal [SURVEY, secondhand]: per an ABA survey cited by Clio, 46% of law firms and only 14% of solo attorneys report having a marketing budget at all. Consultants recommend 2-5% of gross revenue (unverified at primary ABA source; ABA survey year not confirmed). — [Clio](https://www.clio.com/blog/marketing-budget-for-small-law-firm/)
- Legal [VENDOR]: solo and boutique firms advised to spend 5-7% of revenue; one vendor says new or solo firms may need 12-20%. Vendor advice, not measured (unverified). — [GrowLaw](https://growlaw.co/blog/law-firm-marketing-budget)
- Dental [SURVEY, small/vendor]: Mouthwatch 2025 dental marketing trends report: 38.8% of practices spend $1-$1,000 per month on marketing and 13.2% spend $0 (unverified at primary; sample size not seen). — [Mouthwatch](https://www.mouthwatch.com/resources/dental-marketing-trends-2025)
- Dental [VENDOR/OPINION]: recommended ranges conflict: 3-6% of revenue ([Hip Agency](https://hip.agency/dental-marketing-budget-guide-investing-the-right-amount-in-growth/)), 4-7% ([Best Results Dental Marketing](https://bestresultsdentalmarketing.com/?p=3548)), 7-10% for growth-focused practices ([Oral Health Group](https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/features/how-much-should-dental-practices-spend-on-marketing/)).

### Inferences
- If a third of small businesses have under $1,000 a month in total marketing budget and micro-businesses skew under $500, then Mirror's $495 entry price is the entire budget for a large part of the solo and 2-10 employee market. The $995 and $1,995 tiers only fit businesses that are already in the "above $1,000 a month" two-thirds.
- Only about half of dental practices (38.8% + 13.2% = 52%) spend more than $1,000 a month, and only 14% of solo attorneys even have a budget. Affordability differs more by firm size than by trade.
- 61% of small businesses do not buy SEO at all. A service adjacent to SEO is selling into a market where most owners have not yet paid for the older version of the idea.

### Gaps
- No primary 2024-2026 survey found giving median dollars per month by exact size band (solo vs 2-10 vs 11-50). LocaliQ only gives relative likelihoods.
- No measured (as opposed to recommended) marketing spend found for restaurants and food, fitness, beauty/salons, or accountants and other professional services. Not available from sources reached.
- No ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Jobber benchmark report on marketing spend was found; searches returned only agency blogs.
- A "6% of revenue average for HVACR contractors, per an ACCA study" figure appeared in search summaries but I could not pin it to a specific page, so it is not cited.
- Constant Contact Small Business Now, Taradel, Clutch, Vendasta, CallRail and Thryv survey reports were not reached.

## 2. What do they pay for the closest comparable services, and what do mass-market vendors charge?

### Takeaway
The mass-market entry point for small-business marketing help sits around $150 to $300 a month for tools and listings, and $500 to $1,000 a month for a human-run SEO retainer. Mirror's $495 entry matches the low end of an agency retainer, not the entry tier of the mass-market vendors.

### Cited Findings
SEO and local SEO retainers
- [SURVEY] SE Ranking agency pricing survey (260 agencies: 170 respondents plus 90 from its catalog; 94% serve small or local businesses; article dated Dec 13, 2024; mixed North America and Europe): 30% charge under $500 a month, 64% charge under $1,000, 20% charge $1,000-$2,000, 13% charge $2,000-$5,000, 2% over $5,000. — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)
- [SURVEY] Same survey: 66% charge $500-$2,000 per one-off project; 50% of agencies keep clients 2+ years (these charge the most); 13% keep clients under 6 months (this group charges no more than $1,000 a month). — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)
- [SURVEY, all segments] Ahrefs 2024 survey of 439 SEO providers: average monthly retainer $3,209 for agencies, $1,348 for freelancers. Not small-business specific (unverified at primary). — [Morningscore](https://morningscore.io/how-much-should-seo-cost-for-a-small-business/)
- [VENDOR] Local SEO guides: $300-$900 a month for small campaigns, $1,000-$3,000 for larger ones ([OneNine](https://onenine.com/local-seo-pricing/)); $500 to $2,000+ ([SeoProfy](https://seoprofy.com/blog/local-seo-pricing)).

Google Business Profile, care plans, social
- [VENDOR] GBP management: $300-$1,200 a month from one Colorado agency, with full map-pack management at $800-$1,200. — [Colorado Web Impressions](https://coloradowebimpressions.com/answers/how-much-does-google-business-profile-management-cost)
- [VENDOR] GBP management via Merchynt: $125-$400 a month per profile plus about $499 one-time setup; bundled local SEO (GBP + citations + reporting) typically $300-$800 a month. — [SlashPost](https://slashpost.ai/blogs/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-optimization-cost)
- [VENDOR] Website care plans: from $79 to $99 a month at sampled providers (unverified; small sample of vendor pages). — [Blake Strategies Group](https://blakestrategiesgroup.com/services)
- [VENDOR] Social media management retainers: $500 to $5,000+ a month in 2026. — [Sked Social](https://skedsocial.com/blog/what-to-charge-for-social-media-services.md)

Named mass-market vendors (all third-party reported; none confirmed on the vendor's own pricing page)
- Birdeye: Starter $299, Growth $349, Dominate $449 per month per location (2026, aggregator). — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye)
- Podium: $399-$599 a month (aggregator estimate; G2 shows "Contact us"). — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/compare/birdeye-vs-podium/)
- Thryv: Marketing Center from $244 a month ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/156926/Thryv/pricing/)); a Sept 2025 guide puts bundles at $646 to $1,475 a month ([Zeeg](https://zeeg.me/en/blog/post/thryv-pricing)). The two conflict; likely different products.
- Yelp Ads: start at $150 a month; Upgrade Package $180; bundle from $270; self-serve, no term contract (2026). Agencies recommend $1,000-$1,500 a month to see results, which is advice, not a minimum. — [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/)
- Scorpion: no published prices. A competitor reports $1,500 to $10,000+ a month in management fees plus ad spend, setup fees of $5,000-$50,000 and 12-24 month contracts. Competitor claim; treat with caution. — [Rebolt](https://rebolthq.com/alternatives/scorpion-alternatives)
- Angi Ads: no published pricing. — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/angi-ads/pricing)
- GoDaddy: self-serve SEO tools roughly $5 to $20 a month ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/seo/godaddy-seo-review/)); managed SEO services reported at roughly $299-$499 a month ([HostAdvice](https://hostadvice.com/blog/digital-marketing/seo/is-godaddy-search-engine-visibility-worth-it/)) (unverified).

### Inferences
- Three price clusters exist in what small businesses already buy: under $100 (self-serve tools, care plans), $150-$450 (Yelp, Thryv, Birdeye, Podium, GBP management, managed GoDaddy SEO), and $500-$2,000 (agency SEO and social retainers). Mirror currently has no tier in the middle cluster, which is where the mass-market vendors compete.
- 30% of SEO agencies serving small businesses charge under $500 a month, so a sub-$500 done-for-you tier is normal in this market, not a discount position.
- The SE Ranking link between low price and short tenure (under-6-month clients all pay $1,000 or less) is a warning that a cheap tier may churn faster. It is a correlation only.

### Gaps
- No confirmed list prices for Hibu, Broadly, Housecall Pro marketing add-ons, Wix SEO services, or Angi lead fees. Angi figures found ($0-$100 a month subscription, $5-$40 per lead) came from low-quality SEO pages and are not cited as fact.
- No vendor price was verified on the vendor's own page; all are aggregator or competitor reports.
- No survey of what small businesses actually pay (buyer side) for reputation tools was found; all figures are seller list prices.

## 3. What is a new customer worth, by trade?

### Takeaway
Reliable by-trade customer-value data is poor; the commonly quoted figures (HVAC about $15,000 lifetime, plumbing $8,000-$12,000, dental about $10,000) are vendor numbers, and the one contractor-sourced dataset (2019) is far lower. High-ticket trades can justify a $500+ monthly fee from one or two extra jobs; no data was found to support that for restaurants, bakeries or salons.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, no method; article dated Apr 29, 2026] Average ticket / claimed lifetime value: HVAC $1,850 / $15,340; plumbing $950 / $8,200-$12,000; roofing $8,000-$15,000 / $12,000-$18,000; electrical $1,200 / $6,500-$8,000; general contractor $25,000-$100,000 / $30,000-$75,000; house cleaning $250-$400 / $3,500-$6,000; painting $3,000-$8,000 / $6,000-$10,000. The page does not source the table; the HVAC figure is linked to a firm called CFOx. — [Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/home-services-marketing-benchmarks-by-trade/)
- [VENDOR] Same page, blended cost per lead: HVAC $149, plumbing $127, roofing $124, electrical $142, general contractor $425; roofing quote-to-close 40-50%. — [Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/home-services-marketing-benchmarks-by-trade/)
- [Contractor data, OLD: 2019] AHR Expo 2019 session: average lifetime value $2,679 for an HVAC customer and $1,055 for a plumbing customer; contractors tend to overestimate lifetime value; 60% of HVAC and 73% of plumbing customer revenue arrives in the first year. Flagged as older than 2024 (unverified at page level). — [Contractor Magazine](https://www.contractormag.com/management/ahr-2019-session-low-cost-leads-are-hurting-your-business)
- [VENDOR] The $15,340 HVAC lifetime figure is built from a first-year repair, a later system replacement and referrals. — [WhatConverts](https://www.whatconverts.com/blog/the-hvac-customer-lifetime-value-gap-why-153-cpl-is-actually-cheap/)
- [VENDOR] Dental patient lifetime value commonly put near $10,000, range about $4,500 to $22,000 (unverified; originating page not confirmed). — [Clicks Geek](https://clicksgeek.com/?p=17382)
- [VENDOR, worked example not survey] Gym: a $165 a month member staying about 3.3 years gives roughly $6,534 lifetime value; high-end studios above $400 a month (unverified). — [source page](https://kompas.wasmer.app/https_www_linkedin_com/top-content/ecommerce/implementing-a-loyalty-program-for-shoppers/member-lifetime-value-calculation)
- [VENDOR] Salons and spas: annual client retention 60-70%; membership clients retain 2-3x better. No dollar lifetime value given. — [SchedulingKit](https://www.schedulingkit.com/statistics/client-lifetime-value-statistics)

### Inferences
- Break-even on revenue alone (not profit) at $495 a month ($5,940 a year): about 3 HVAC jobs or 6 plumbing jobs a year at the vendor ticket figures, or under one roofing job. On profit the number of jobs needed is several times higher; margins were not researched.
- Using the conservative 2019 figures instead ($1,055 lifetime per plumbing customer), a plumber needs roughly 6 new customers a year just to cover $495 a month in revenue terms. The pitch works for trades but is not a one-customer payback except for roofers, contractors and lawyers.
- Low-ticket trades (bakery, restaurant, salon) would need many more incremental customers per month to cover even $495; with no measured lifetime values, the report should not claim payback for them.

### Gaps
- Personal injury and family law case values: not found. Clio Legal Trends data was not reached.
- Restaurant and bakery average check and lifetime value: nothing reliable found.
- Salon dollar lifetime value: not found (retention only).
- No ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Jobber primary data on average ticket was reached; all trade ticket figures are from an unsourced agency table.
- Gross margins by trade were not researched, so payback figures above are revenue-based only.

## 4. How much are small-business owners using or worried about AI search in 2026?

### Takeaway
Awareness is high but action is low: in May 2026 only 13% of US small-business owners were actively optimizing for AI search and 22% had never heard of it, yet nearly half say a competitor showing up first would make it a top priority. No survey measured what owners would pay.

### Cited Findings
- [SURVEY] Bluehost State of Small Business AI Confidence (N=350 US small-business owners, 1-50 employees, fielded May 2026 by ListenLabs, margin of error ±5.2 points; respondents: 38% solo, 44% 2-10 employees, 18% 11-50): on AI search optimization, 13% actively optimizing, 35% exploring with no action, 31% heard of it but don't know how, 22% first heard of it in the survey. 87% not yet acting. — [Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/state-of-small-business-ai-confidence)
- [SURVEY] Same study: 47% would make it a top priority if a competitor showed up first in AI search results; 78% want a competitor visibility report showing how competitors appear in AI results; 13% say they already get traffic from AI search; 87% use AI tools themselves; 56% pay for at least one AI tool; cost is the top concern for only 5%. — [Bluehost](https://www.bluehost.com/state-of-small-business-ai-confidence)
- [SURVEY] LocaliQ: nearly 60% of small businesses use AI for some marketing (content 52%, social 39%, chatbots 34%). This is use of AI as a tool, not AI visibility. — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)
- [SURVEY, 2025, mixed audience] Adobe: 76% of business owners and marketers said appearing in ChatGPT answers is essential for their brand in 2025; 47% use ChatGPT for marketing. Sample includes marketers, not just small-business owners (unverified at primary). — [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/chatgpt-as-a-search-engine?afsrc=1)

### Inferences
- The Bluehost finding that 78% want a competitor AI visibility report supports a free scan as the lead offer, and the 47% "competitor shows up first" trigger suggests the scan should show a named competitor, not just the owner's own results.
- With 53% either unaware or unsure how to act, the sale is partly education. That usually lengthens sales cycles and favors a low-commitment first paid step.
- Bluehost sells websites and hosting, so its framing of results may favor "your website matters"; the raw percentages are still usable.

### Gaps
- No survey found on stated willingness to pay, or budget set aside, for AI visibility among small businesses.
- No survey found measuring the share of a small business's customers who came from ChatGPT or similar, other than the 13% of owners who say they get some AI traffic.
- No by-trade breakdown of owner concern.

## 5. How many consumers use AI assistants to find local businesses?

### Takeaway
Two independent early-2026 US surveys land in the same place: about 45-47% of US adults used generative AI to find or get recommendations for local businesses, up from 6% a year earlier in BrightLocal's tracking. Nearly all still verify on Google or the business website.

### Cited Findings
- [SURVEY] BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (published Feb 11, 2026; 1,002 US adults, representative panel): use of ChatGPT and other generative AI for local business recommendations rose from 6% to 45% in a year, making it the third most used source behind Google and Facebook. — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)
- [SURVEY] Same survey: 40% trust AI platforms for business recommendations vs 32% who do not; 42% trust AI recommendations as much as traditional reviews; 82% read AI-generated review summaries and 23% would rely on them alone. — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)
- [SURVEY] Yext Consumer Search Behaviors Report 2026 (1,120 US adults, fielded March 2026; a "sneak peek" of a fuller report): 47% of US adults used AI for local search in the past month; 59% among daily local searchers; highest among ages 30-44 at 54%. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)
- [SURVEY] Yext by category: AI adoption for local search ranges from 52% for restaurants to 37% for automotive. No other categories published. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)
- [SURVEY] Yext on trust and checking: 74% of AI users rate trust in AI local recommendations 4 or 5 out of 5; 93%+ take at least one verification step (62% search Google, 58% visit the business website, 52% click cited sources); only 7% act with no further research. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)
- [SURVEY] Yext on non-users: 53% have not used AI for local search; 43% of them are likely to try within six months. 48% of AI users aged 18-29 say they use it less than a year ago. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/resources/consumer-search-behaviors-findings)
- Sample note: another Yext page describes a 3,848-adult five-country sample with 44% global usage; the US-only figure is 47%. Yext itself says findings are directional. — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/go/explore-consumer-search-behaviors-data-2026/)

### Inferences
- The 6% to 45% jump in one year is large enough that question wording may have changed between waves; treat the direction as solid and the size of the jump with some caution.
- Because 58% of AI users then visit the business website and 62% check Google, wrong facts in AI answers are likely to be noticed against the real listing. That supports Mirror's "correct the facts at the source" framing.
- Restaurants show the highest AI use (52%), but they are also the trade with the weakest evidence of ability to pay.

### Gaps
- SOCi, Pew and Gartner figures specific to local business discovery were not found.
- No trade-level adoption figures beyond restaurants and automotive.
- Neither survey measures completed purchases that began in an AI assistant.

## 6. Conversion norms for free audits into paid retainers, and churn norms

### Takeaway
There is no reliable benchmark for free-audit-to-paid conversion; published figures range from under 1% to 80% and are anecdotes. Churn data is better: agency retainers lose roughly 18-32% of clients a year, and subscriptions under $100 a month churn at a median 6-9% a month versus 3-4% above $500.

### Cited Findings
Free audit conversion (all weak)
- [OPINION] A free audit typically converts at 5-15% on opt-in (visitor to request), but yields better pipeline than a quiz with a higher opt-in rate (unverified; agency blog). — [Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/how-to-get-ai-agency-clients-with-a-free-audit)
- [VENDOR] Insites, which sells audit software, claims 10x higher conversion for campaigns that led with an audit for one customer; its calculator's 33-50% conversion figures are modelled assumptions. — [Insites](https://insites.com/roi-calculator/)
- [Anecdote] An unnamed New York agency claims 80% conversion from free 15-minute brand audits to paying clients; no method. Discount heavily. — [BusySeed](https://www.busyseed.com/lead-magnets-that-actually-work-going-beyond-the-free-ebook)

Agency retainer churn
- [VENDOR study] Focus Digital (analysis Sept-Nov 2025): retainer-based agencies about 1.6% monthly / 18% annual churn; project-based about 4.2% monthly / 42% annual; by service line, full-service lowest at about 25% annual and PPC highest at about 49% (unverified at primary). — [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/?p=6135)
- [VENDOR study] Predictable Profits 2025 Agency Growth Benchmark: retainer agencies show about 80%+ annual retention; 70% is the red-flag level (unverified). — [Agiled](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics)
- [VENDOR study] The Starr Conspiracy: median B2B agency client retention 68% year over year, top quartile 85%+. B2B, not small local businesses (unverified). — [The Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/benchmarks/b2b-marketing-agency-benchmarks-2025)
- [SURVEY] SE Ranking: 50% of SEO agencies keep clients 2+ years; 13% keep them under 6 months, and that group charges $1,000 a month or less. — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)

Subscription churn
- [DATASET] ProfitWell: with average revenue per account under $100 a month, monthly gross dollar churn runs 3-16% with a median of 6-9%; above $500 a month it runs about 2-6% with a median near 3-4% (benchmarks from 2018-2021 data; older than 2024, flagged; unverified at primary). — [ProfitWell](https://profitwell.com/blog/average-revenue-churn-rate-benchmarks)
- [DATASET] ProfitWell study of 3,000+ subscription companies (2018, older): companies with more annual contracts had significantly lower churn regardless of price; 20-40% of churn came from failed card payments (unverified at primary). — [Paddle / ProfitWell](https://paddle.com/studios/shows/profitwell-report/largest-study-on-churn)
- [Aggregator] SMB software monthly churn commonly cited at 3-7% (30-58% annual), attributed to Recurly and Paddle 2025 data. — [Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate); a lower range of 1.5-3.0% monthly logo churn for accounts under $15K a year is given by [PipelineRoad](https://pipelineroad.com/agency/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks). Sources conflict.

### Inferences
- A planning assumption consistent with the evidence: a done-for-you retainer at $500+ a month should expect roughly 2-4% monthly churn; a sub-$300 tier sold to solo businesses should expect 5-8% monthly. This is my synthesis of the ranges above, not a published benchmark.
- Price and churn move in opposite directions in every dataset found. A cheaper entry tier widens the audience but should be modelled with roughly double the churn of the higher tiers.
- Failed card payments causing 20-40% of churn means card retry and reminder emails are a cheap retention lever regardless of price.
- Since no trustworthy audit-conversion benchmark exists, Mirror should track its own scan-to-call and call-to-paid rates from the first 50-100 scans rather than plan around an external number.

### Gaps
- No measured benchmark for free audit / free scan to paid monthly retainer conversion. A "11% for audit requesters vs 1% for ebook downloaders" example appeared in search summaries but I could not pin it to a page and it looked like an illustration, so it is not cited.
- No data on trial-free service retainers specifically.
- No churn data specific to marketing services sold to local small businesses by trade.
- Recurly and ChartMogul primary reports were not opened.

## 7. Price thresholds that matter to small-business owners

### Takeaway
I found no hard evidence for specific thresholds such as $100, $300, $500 or $1,000 a month, or for a "card without partner sign-off" limit. What exists is indirect: where budgets cluster and where vendors set entry prices.

### Cited Findings
- [SURVEY, indirect] A third of small businesses have a total marketing budget under $1,000 a month and the smallest skew under $500, which sets a practical ceiling on any single service. — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/small-business-marketing-trends-report/)
- [SURVEY, indirect] 38.8% of dental practices spend $1-$1,000 a month on all marketing (unverified). — [Mouthwatch](https://www.mouthwatch.com/resources/dental-marketing-trends-2025)
- [Market evidence] Vendor entry prices cluster just under round numbers: Yelp Ads $150, Thryv $244, Birdeye $299, Podium $399, Birdeye top tier $449 (third-party reported). — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/156926/Thryv/pricing/), [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/)
- [SURVEY, indirect] 64% of SEO agencies serving small businesses price under $1,000 a month and 30% under $500. — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)
- [SURVEY, intro only seen] Intuit study of 4,000+ US service SMBs: resistance to "yet another monthly bill" is one of the biggest barriers to adopting new apps (unverified beyond the introduction; date not confirmed). — [Intuit](https://blogs.a.intuit.com/?p=29363)

### Inferences
- The clustering of mass-market vendors at $244-$449 suggests those sellers have found that the sub-$500 band is where owner-operators buy without a long sales process. This is inference from price lists, not from buyer research.
- $495 sits at the top of that band and $995 just under the level where 64% of small-business SEO retainers stop. Mirror's two lower prices are already on recognizable thresholds; what is missing is a tier in the $150-$300 band where Yelp, Thryv and Birdeye start.
- Any statement that "owners can put up to $X on a card without asking a partner" would be practitioner opinion; I found no source for it.

### Gaps
- No survey or experiment found on psychological price ceilings for small-business monthly services.
- No evidence found on sign-off or card-approval thresholds in small firms.
- No Van Westendorp or similar willingness-to-pay study for small-business marketing or AI visibility services.

## 8. What pricing researchers and practitioners say about tiers, entry tiers, setup fees and prepay discounts

### Takeaway
I could verify only general guidance: three tiers with the middle as the target, annual prepay discounts of about 15-25%, and annual billing lowering churn. I could not reach primary material from Hormozi, Kyle Poyar, a16z or Lenny Rachitsky on these specific questions, so those are gaps, not findings.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Good-better-best: the basic tier draws price-sensitive buyers, the middle tier is where most customers should land, and the top tier acts as an anchor that makes the middle look like a good deal; limit to three or four tiers to avoid choice paralysis (secondary explainer). — [Seonio](https://seonio.com/wiki/good-better-best-pricing)
- [Secondhand attribution] Annual plan discounts typically 15-25%, attributed to Price Intelligently; a 15-20% "sweet spot" attributed to ProfitWell. Neither confirmed at the primary source. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-structure-the-perfect-annual-discount-a-guide-for-saas-leaders)
- [OPINION] The right annual discount depends on churn and acquisition cost: 25-30% can make sense with high monthly churn and high acquisition cost; 10-15% when churn is already low. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/whats-the-optimal-discount-for-annual-developer-subscriptions-a-data-driven-approach)
- [DATASET, older 2018] ProfitWell: a higher share of annual contracts goes with significantly lower churn at every price level, because the customer makes one buying decision a year instead of twelve (unverified at primary). — [Paddle / ProfitWell](https://paddle.com/studios/shows/profitwell-report/largest-study-on-churn)
- [Market evidence on setup fees] Setup fees are normal in this market: Merchynt about $499 one-time on GBP management ([SlashPost](https://slashpost.ai/blogs/google-business-profile/google-business-profile-optimization-cost)); Scorpion reportedly $5,000-$50,000 with 12-24 month contracts, per a competitor ([Rebolt](https://rebolthq.com/alternatives/scorpion-alternatives)). By contrast Yelp Ads self-serve has no contract ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/)).
- [Reference only] Kyle Poyar's "Your pricing is (probably) broken" exists but is paywalled; content not read. — [Growth Unhinged](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/your-pricing-is-broken)

### Inferences
- Combining the churn evidence (low price means higher churn) with the annual-billing evidence (prepay lowers churn), a low entry tier is safest when paired with a quarterly or annual prepay option. This is my synthesis, not a quoted recommendation.
- Because Mirror's first months involve front-loaded correction work, a one-time setup fee or a minimum term has market precedent (Merchynt, Scorpion), but the mass-market trend (Yelp, Hibu described as month-to-month) is toward no contract. Which fits better depends on the tier.

- a16z and Lenny Rachitsky on pricing for services sold to small businesses: not searched successfully; nothing found.
- No evidence specific to quarterly prepay.
- No study of a low entry tier acting as a "conveyor belt" to upgrades in small-business services; upgrade-rate data was not found.
